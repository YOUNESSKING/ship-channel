# First 5 videos: plan, opening sample, cost and time

All facts below are draft and must pass the fact sheet + 3 checkers before the script is final.
Every topic passes the gate: famous ship, not covered yet, surprising answer, not a museum answer.
Order spreads clusters apart (no repeat harbour/cluster within ~3 months).

## 1. USS Oriskany: "Why the Navy Sank Its Own Aircraft Carrier"
**Publish by Nov 11** (SS United States reef sinking news hook, Nov 12). Thumbnail text: **SUNK ON PURPOSE**.

**Opening sample (draft):**
> Twenty-four miles off the Florida coast, two hundred feet down, lies an aircraft carrier longer than
> three football fields. No enemy put her there. The United States Navy did, on purpose, with explosives
> it placed itself. And this month, a second famous American ship is about to join her.

**Plan:** reef site map (sea-floor look) → May 2006 sinking (Navy film, ~37 min to go under) → why not
scrap her (options struck out: museum / scrap / reef) → toxic clean-up (hull cutaway) → Korea/Vietnam
service told as flashbacks, not a résumé → what divers find today → reversal: is a reef worth it? →
SS United States ending → closing question.
**Footage:** US Navy sinking film (DVIDS/NARA), NHHC photos, NOAA, Florida FWC reef footage (check rights).
**New builds:** sea-floor map look, carrier silhouette, sinking animation, ship SFX (one-time, reused later).

## 2. Mallows Bay: "100+ Ships Are Rotting 30 Miles From the White House"
Thumbnail text: **GHOST FLEET**.
> Just down the river from the White House, the water is full of ships. Not one or two: more than a
> hundred, built in a hurry for a war, and then burned and abandoned where they float. Most people in
> Washington have never heard of them.

**Plan:** map Potomac → DC → wreck field from above → 1917 emergency shipbuilding → why they were
useless when the war ended → salvage fire → nature takes over → marine sanctuary today.
**Footage:** NOAA sanctuary photos, Library of Congress, USGS aerials. Strong map video.

## 3. USS Nevada: "The Battleship That Survived Two Atomic Bombs"
Viewer request (747 likes under Forgotten Fleet). Thumbnail text: **SURVIVED 2 A-BOMBS**.
> On December 7th, one battleship tried to escape Pearl Harbor while it burned. Five years later the
> Navy painted her orange and put her at the centre of an atomic bomb test. She did not sink. What the
> Navy did next was even harder to believe.

**Plan:** Battleship Row map → the run down the channel → D-Day → Bikini target (orange ship) → two
blasts → towed off Hawaii, sunk 1948 → wreck found 2020, ~15,000 ft.
**Footage:** Operation Crossroads film (NARA, public domain), NHHC.

## 4. USS Ranger: "The Top Gun Carrier Sold for One Cent"
Thumbnail text: **SOLD FOR 1¢**.
> She was the deck in Top Gun. She carried thousands of sailors through two wars. And when the Navy
> was finished with her, it sold her for exactly one cent.

**Plan:** film bridge (careful: no film clips, only Navy footage of the real ship) → why the Navy pays
scrappers rather than gets paid → the save-the-Ranger museum campaign that failed → the tow to Texas →
what's left. **Footage:** Navy/DVIDS. Rule: no copyrighted movie footage, titles/names only.

## 5. USS Samuel B. Roberts: "The Deepest Shipwreck Ever Found"
Viewer request. Thumbnail text: **22,000 FT DOWN**.
> Nearly seven kilometres under the Philippine Sea, a small American warship sits in the dark. She was
> one of the smallest ships in the battle, and she attacked a fleet of battleships anyway.

**Plan:** depth gauge animation (anim_gauge) from surface to wreck → Battle off Samar map → "destroyer
escort that fought like a battleship" → sinking → 2022 discovery → what the cameras saw.
**Footage:** NHHC, NARA; the 2022 dive footage is owned by Caladan Oceanic (not usable, describe only).

Swapped out from the topic list: Down Periscope (USS Pampanito) and Taney are museum ships (DON'T rule);
"USS Johnston deepest wreck" is outdated (Samuel B. Roberts was found deeper in 2022).
