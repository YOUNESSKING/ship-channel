## 1. The stakes (cold open material)

On 17 May 2006 the US Navy blew holes in one of its own aircraft carriers on purpose, off Pensacola, Florida. It
went down in 37 minutes, upright, into about 212 ft of water. It was the largest ship ever sunk deliberately to make
an artificial reef. It went down knowing that roughly 700 lb of PCBs were still on board, under a one-off EPA
permit. Twenty years later, the same coast (Okaloosa County, next door) is about to sink an even bigger ship, the
990-ft SS United States, which will take the "world's largest artificial reef" title from Oriskany.
- Navy release, 17 May 2006 (NNS060517-05, NAVSEA PA), mirrored: https://www.globalsecurity.org/military/library/news/2006/05/mil-060517-nns01.htm [NAVSEA]
- EPA approval, 15 Feb 2006: https://www.epa.gov/archive/epapages/newsroom_archive/newsreleases/0596ba557b46787b852571170062cf36.html [EPA]
- Okaloosa County release, 26 Oct 2026: https://myokaloosa.com/sites/default/files/Users/piouser/SSUSDEPLOYMENT-NOV12.pdf [GOV]

## 2. Name and birth: a WWII carrier finished too late for WWII

- Named for the 1777 Battle of Oriskany (Saratoga campaign, Mohawk Valley, NY). The name was first given to CV-18,
  which was renamed *Wasp* on 26 Sep 1942. NHHC DANFS: https://www.history.navy.mil/research/histories/ship-histories/danfs/o/oriskany.html [NHHC]
- Laid down 1 May 1944, New York Navy Yard, Brooklyn; launched 13 Oct 1945 (after VJ-Day); sponsor Mrs. Ida D.
  Cannon. [NHHC] Same dates in Navsource: http://www.navsource.net/archives/02/34.htm
- Construction suspended 12 Aug 1947 while she was still incomplete. She sat "in a state of preservation" until
  the Korean War began in June 1950, then was "rushed to completion". GlobalSecurity (quotes DANFS text):
  https://www.globalsecurity.org/military/agency/navy/cv-34.htm ; uscarriers: https://www.uscarriers.net/cv34history.htm
- She was finished to a redesigned standard, SCB-27 / "27A" (stronger deck, bigger elevators, more powerful
  catapults for jets). She is described as the first ship built to the 27A design, the template later used to
  modernise other Essex-class ships. Navysite: "first of nine carriers modernized under Project 27A":
  https://www.navysite.de/cv/cv34.htm . Navsource photo caption: drydock "being prepared for the first SCB-27A
  modernization". *Treat "template for 14 other Essex ships" as UNVERIFIED (Wikipedia-derived wording).*
- Commissioned 25 Sep 1950 at New York Naval Shipyard, Capt. Percy H. Lyon commanding. Fleet Admiral Nimitz attended
  (Navsource photo NS023474). [NHHC] [Navsource]
- She was the **last Essex-class carrier completed** (Navsource; warbirdsresourcegroup). GlobalSecurity: "the last
  Essex in commission as a warship" when she went to reserve in 1976.
- DANFS figures as built: 27,100 tons, 888 ft, beam 147'6" (flight deck), draft 31 ft, 33 kn, crew 3,460, 80+
  aircraft. [NHHC] Note: the 911-ft figure (FWC, EPA BMP, Navysite) is the overall length after the 1957–59 rebuild.
  The Navy's own 2006 sinking release still says "888-foot ship". See FACT_SHEET.
- 1957–59 second rebuild (SCB-125A) at San Francisco Naval Shipyard: decommissioned 2 Jan 1957, angled deck,
  enclosed "hurricane bow", new CIC. Recommissioned Mar 1959. [NHHC] Dates also: http://gonavy.jp/CV-CV34.html

## 3. Korea (1952–53): the secret dogfight

- Mediterranean cruise 1951, then to the Pacific. Combat off Korea from late 1952 with CVG-102/CVG-12 (Navsource:
  CVG-12 squadrons deployed 15 Sep 1952 – 18 May 1953). Redesignated CVA-34 on 1 Oct 1952. [Navsource]
- **18 Nov 1952:** Oriskany F9F-5 Panthers (VF-781) met seven MiG-15s that had come out of the Soviet Union near
  Vladivostok. The ship's own action report says two MiGs shot down and two badly damaged, and credits Lt E. R.
  (Royce) Williams and Ltjg J. D. Middleton with one each. NHHC action report:
  https://www.history.navy.mil/content/dam/nhhc/research/archives/action-reports/Korean%20War%20-%20Carrier%20Combat/PDF%27s/cva34a-52.pdf [NHHC]
  NHHC shoot-down list (App. 33): https://www.history.navy.mil/content/dam/nhhc/research/histories/naval-aviation/pdf/app33.pdf
- Later claims (US House bills H.R. 5909, 2021, and H.R. 1819, 2025) say Williams alone shot down "four, very likely
  five" Soviet MiG-15s in a 35-minute fight, and that the fight was kept secret. Russia released the names of four
  pilots after the Cold War. https://www.congress.gov/119/bills/hr1819/BILLS-119hr1819ih.pdf [GOV] Williams received the
  Navy Cross upgrade in 2023 (*UNVERIFIED here; not checked in this pass*). **Script note:** say "officially credited
  with one at the time; later evidence suggests four". Do not state four as settled fact.
- A bomb that broke loose on deck in March 1953 killed two (Navysite says 6 Mar 1953, 2 killed, 13 injured;
  uscarriers says 15 injured; **casualty count conflict**).
- Hollywood: MGM's *Men of the Fighting Lady* used the ship in 1952–53, and in fall 1953 Paramount flew William Holden
  and the *Bridges at Toko-Ri* crew aboard her in the Yellow Sea. USNI Naval History, Aug 2013:
  https://www.usni.org/magazines/naval-history-magazine/2013/july/navys-aerial-oscar [USNI] Toko-Ri is also cited in the
  Florida Senate bill analysis (2008). [GOV]

## 4. Vietnam (1965–75): Stockdale, the fire, McCain

- **Stockdale:** Cdr. James B. Stockdale, commander of Carrier Air Wing 16 aboard Oriskany, was shot down in an A-4
  over North Vietnam on 9 Sep 1965. He was a POW until 12 Feb 1973 and later received the Medal of Honor. NHHC bio:
  https://www.history.navy.mil/research/library/biographical-files/modern-biographical-files-ndl/modern-bios-s/stockdale-james-bond.html [NHHC] ;
  USNA: https://www.usna.edu/Notables/Bios/1947stockdale.php
- 1965 cruise: about 12,000 combat sorties, about 10,000 tons of ordnance (uscarriers/DANFS text). Navy Unit Commendation.
- **The fire, 26 Oct 1966 (Gulf of Tonkin, Yankee Station):** a mishandled magnesium parachute flare ignited in the
  forward ready-service flare locker (hangar bay 1, starboard). It burned at about 5,400 °F and set off "roughly 700
  other flares". The blast "instantly" killed five sailors, set two helicopters on fire and sent flaming debris into
  the forward officer berthing. The fire took about 7 hours to beat (DANFS; Navysite says 3 hours to control). Sailors
  jettisoned bombs over the side by hand. [NHHC]
  - **Death toll: 44 is the standard figure** (DANFS photo captions; Navsource; uscarriers; National Interest). The
    DANFS narrative's own tally says "35 officers (many of them pilots) and 8 enlisted men" = **43**, with 38 injured.
    ThoughtCo also says 43. **Use "44" in the script (the Navy's captioned figure and the memorial list) but flag it
    for the fact-checkers.** Many of the dead were pilots who had flown strikes over Vietnam hours earlier.
  - Date conflict: DANFS says 26 Oct; the older DANFS-derived text (uscarriers, GlobalSecurity, HistoryCentral) says
    27 Oct. 26 Oct is local ship time and is the date on the NHHC captions. Use **26 Oct 1966**.
  - Repairs at San Francisco Bay Naval Shipyard, 23 Nov 1966 – 23 Mar 1967. [NHHC]
- **McCain:** On 26 Oct 1967, exactly one year after the fire, Lt Cdr John S. McCain III (VA-163, A-4E) was shot down
  over Hanoi by an SA-2 missile on his 23rd mission. He was a POW until March 1973. DANFS: "Another two aircraft fell
  to SA-2s south of Hanoi on 26 October ... Both Lt.Cmdr. John S. McCain and Lt(jg) Charles D. Rice remained in
  enemy captivity until March 1973." [NHHC]; LA Times 2006 (ship "which carried ... John McCain"):
  https://www.latimes.com/archives/la-xpm-2006-may-10-na-oriskany10-story.html ; National Interest:
  https://nationalinterest.org/blog/buzz/uss-oriskany-last-essex-class-aircraft-carrier-fought-everywhere-207732
  - DANFS says "south of Hanoi". McCain's own accounts put the shoot-down over Hanoi itself, near the Yen Phu power
    plant, landing in Truc Bach Lake (*UNVERIFIED in this pass*).
  - The same 1967 cruise: Oriskany sent medical help to the burning *Forrestal* (Jul 1967). Air Wing 16 had one of
    the highest loss rates of the war; National Interest cites half its aircraft, 20 pilots killed and 9 captured
    (*single secondary source; UNVERIFIED*).
- Nicknames: "Mighty O", "O-Boat", "Toasted O" (the last one after the fire; TogetherWeServed; *UNVERIFIED*).
- 1972: collision with ammunition ship *Nitro* (28 Jun) and a lost propeller (20 Jul). [Navysite]
- Battle stars: 2 for Korea (consistent). Vietnam: **5** (GlobalSecurity, older DANFS) vs **10** (National Interest,
  Wikipedia). **Conflict: avoid the number.**

## 5. Retirement and a 30-year limbo (1976–2006)

- Last WestPac cruise ended 3 Mar 1976; marked for inactivation 15 Apr 1976; **decommissioned 30 Sep 1976**. Laid up at
  Bremerton, WA. [NHHC] (Navysite and uscarriers wrongly say 30 Sep 1975 in one place; gonavy.jp says 20 Sep 1976.
  Use the DANFS date.)
- Early 1980s: the Reagan-era Navy looked at reactivating her. Congress refused because of her poor condition. The
  GAO (20 Apr 1981) estimated $520M (*figure from the Wikipedia citation of the GAO report; the original was not
  retrieved; UNVERIFIED*).
- **Struck from the Navy list 25 Jul 1989.** [NHHC; GlobalSecurity]
- **Failed museum:** NHHC says "Initially intended for possible sale to Japan as a museum ship". GlobalSecurity: a US
  non-profit pushed legislation to transfer her to the "City of America" project (Japanese businessmen planned to show
  her in Tokyo Bay; Ocala Star-Banner, 19 Jun 1991). It failed for lack of money. No other group asked for her. Four
  Essex sisters were already museums (Yorktown, Intrepid, Hornet, Lexington). [NHHC] [GlobalSecurity]
- **Failed scrapping, twice:**
  1. Sold for scrap 26 Jan 1993 (GlobalSecurity; Navsource says June 1994; LA Times says 1994). The buyer defaulted and
     the ship never left Navy custody.
  2. 9 or 29 Sep 1995 (sources disagree on the day): DRMS sold scrapping rights to **Pegasus International**, a start-up
     at the former Mare Island Navy Yard, Vallejo, CA. She was towed from Bremerton to San Francisco (1 May 1996), then
     to Mare Island. The contract was terminated for default (1 Jul 1997 per GlobalSecurity; 30 Jul 1997 per
     Navysite/uscarriers). LA Times: "the contractor went bankrupt". DRMS then tried and failed to sell her twice more.
     DANFS: "three subsequent dismantling contracts were cancelled owing to cost and environmental concerns." [NHHC]
- While rusting at Mare Island she was used as a set for *What Dreams May Come* (1998) (Wikipedia and Warbirds
  site only; **UNVERIFIED**).
- **1999: towed about 15,000 miles from California around South America** (LA Times: "around Cape Horn"; Wikipedia:
  "via the Strait of Magellan", **route conflict**) to the MARAD reserve fleet at Beaumont, TX, because the Pacific
  side had no inactive berths. She deteriorated there for four years. Scrapping her under a Navy contract was put at
  **$12M** (GlobalSecurity, quoting the Navy) and **$24M** (USNI 2006). Carrier scrapping was unfunded. [GlobalSecurity] [LA Times]

## 6. Why reef her? The decision (2003–2004)

- The FY2004 National Defense Authorization Act (signed 25 Nov 2003, PL 108-136) let the Navy give ships to states as
  artificial reefs and share the preparation costs. Oriskany was the **first** warship offered. Florida Senate bill
  analysis SB 432 (2008): https://flsenate.gov/Session/Bill/2008/432/Analyses/20080432SEP_2008s0432.ep.pdf [GOV]
- Her **economics**: dismantling cost more than the steel was worth and was unfunded; reefing promised tourism revenue
  and cheap disposal. USNI 2006: the Navy "considers the program a bargain" compared with $24M to scrap.
  https://www.usni.org/magazines/naval-history-magazine/2006/june/naval-history-news [USNI]
- Sep 2003: remediation contract to Resolve Marine Group / ESCO Marine JV (Wikipedia; NAVSEA/MarineLink 2006 confirms
  "Resolve-Esco Joint Venture" as contractor): https://ws15.marinelink.com/news/oriskany-sinking-course310063
- 21 Jan 2004: towed Beaumont → Corpus Christi for cleanup. [GlobalSecurity]
- **5 Apr 2004: Navy picks Florida (Escambia County / Pensacola)** over bids from Mississippi, Texas, South Carolina +
  Georgia jointly, and South Florida. [GlobalSecurity; USNI] The Navy said the Florida plan gave "the best value".
- **Rejected alternative:** a Miami-Dade/Broward bid. Artificial Reefs International later claimed that South Florida
  site would have meant $320M a year and 4,400 jobs (*advocacy estimate, ADV, not used as fact*):
  https://cragenda.broward.org/docs/2016/CCCM/20160607_499/22141_Exhibit%202%20-%20Artifical%20Reef%20Economic%20Impact.pdf

## 7. The cleanup, the PCBs, and the delays (2004–2006)

- What came off: all liquid PCBs, fuel, oil, loose asbestos, loose paint; and, unexpectedly, the **wooden flight
  deck**. USNI: removing contaminants had a $2.1M allocation that "ballooned to more than $7.6 million, at least half
  being spent on the unforeseen removal of the wooden flight deck. Elevated PCBs, the result of fluid used in her
  catapults, caused the $3 million-plus increase." [USNI]
- What stayed: **about 700 lb of solid PCBs** in electrical cable, felt and foam gaskets, insulation and heat-resistant
  paint. The NAVSEA fact sheet (Jan 2006) says 97% of the remaining PCBs were in electrical cabling, and that removing
  it all "without totally dismantling the ex-ORISKANY is virtually impossible and would be extremely costly".
  https://www.navsea.navy.mil/Portals/103/Documents/TeamShips/SEA21/InactiveShips/ArtificialReefing/Ex-Oriskany/ex-ORISKANY_Fact_sheet.pdf [NAVSEA] ;
  EPA release [EPA]. The Basel Action Network puts it at 362,200 lb of cable insulation, 31,700 lb of fiberglass
  bulkhead insulation and more [ADV]: https://georgiastrait.org/wp-content/uploads/2015/02/Jobs_and_Dollars_Overboard_20101.pdf
- The Navy spent **$3.74M** building a PCB leaching / Prospective Risk Assessment Model (PRAM) to show the remaining PCBs
  were low-risk (EPA/MARAD BMP, Exhibit 4). [EPA]
- Timeline of delays (USNI 2006; EPA working paper 2007):
  - Planned sinking summer 2004 → slipped; towed Corpus Christi → Pensacola 15–20 Dec 2004.
  - Rescheduled for 2005, but EPA's PCB permit was still pending. In June 2005 she was towed **back to Texas**
    (Beaumont) to ride out hurricane season, at a cost of **$800,000** (USNI). She survived **Hurricane Rita** (Sep 2005)
    there.
  - Navy applied for EPA risk-based approval June 2005; EPA said in Dec 2005 it intended to approve; public comment
    until 19 Jan 2006. [NAVSEA fact sheet]
  - **15 Feb 2006: EPA Region 4 approves** under TSCA (40 CFR 761.62(c)): "will not pose an unreasonable risk".
    It was the **first risk-based PCB disposal approval for a reef ship**, and it applied only to Oriskany. It *required*
    FWC and Escambia County to monitor PCBs in fish. [EPA]
  - Arrived NAS Pensacola for the last time 22–23 Mar 2006 (USNI: 22 Mar; EPA BMP photo: 23 Mar; uscarriers: 22 Mar).
- Oriskany was also the first ship cleaned to the new EPA/MARAD "Best Management Practices" guidance. The final guidance
  was dated May 2006, the same month she sank. [NAVSEA release; EPA BMP:
  https://archive.epa.gov/water/test/web/pdf/2006_05_17_oceans_habitat_artificialreefsuments_0605finalreefguidance.pdf]

## 9. Sinking day: 17 May 2006

- Towed out 15–16 May. Anchored with a four-point mooring (anchors of 33,000 lb each) so she would sink facing south.
  Ballast tanks were pre-flooded and access plates were cut between engineering spaces so the water spread evenly.
  NAS Pensacola PA via MarineLink, 24 Apr 2006. [NAVSEA]
- A Navy EOD team placed charges on **22 sea-chest pipes and valves** inside the hull a few days earlier and armed
  them that morning. Detonation at **10:25 a.m. CDT**. [NAVSEA release] More than 500 lb of plastic explosives (AP via Fox;
  BBC "500lb"). https://www.foxnews.com/story/navy-sinks-aircraft-carrier-to-create-reef-in-gulf-of-mexico
- **She sank in about 37 minutes** (NAVSEA, AP/LA Times). BBC says "just 30 minutes". Predictions had been up to 5 hours
  (BBC) and AP called it "about 4 1/2 hours faster than predicted". She went down stern first (Wikipedia; 19FortyFive
  says the stern went first, then the bow).
- She settled **upright, bow south, north–south orientation, in about 212 ft** (NAVSEA "approximately 212 feet"; FWC 212 ft;
  USNI pre-sinking "210 feet"; Wikipedia "210 ft"). Ownership passed to Florida **as she touched bottom**. [NAVSEA]
- Hundreds of veterans watched from boats, some of them former crew (AP: "As hundreds of veterans looked on solemnly";
  BBC "dozens"). [AP]
- Location: FWC says **22.5 nautical miles SE of Pensacola Pass**; EPA "approximately 23 miles south"; NAVSEA "approximately
  24 miles off the coast"; FWC 2011 report "23.5 nm"; BBC "26 miles". Uppermost structure at **30°02.555′N, 87°00.397′W**
  (FWC). https://myfwc.com/fishing/saltwater/artificial-reefs/oriskany [FWC]
- Navy line: "the largest ship intentionally sunk as an artificial reef". [NAVSEA]

## 10. On the bottom: depth, storms, collapse

- As sunk: flight deck about 135 ft, island top about 70 ft (Wikipedia citing Barnette 2008, *UNVERIFIED*). Dive
  operators say the plan was a flight deck at ~130 ft, the recreational deep-diving limit (Wet Dream Charters [TRADE]).
- **Settling:** FWC divers found she had **settled about 10 ft into the sediment by 2.5 years after sinking** (FWC 2011
  report). MBT Divers: **Hurricane Gustav (2008)** pushed her about 8 ft into the sand with a slight (~5°) starboard list;
  Ike did nothing more. [FWC] [TRADE] Result: the flight deck is now at **~145–150 ft** (FWC), deeper than the
  recreational limit, so the deck itself is a technical dive.
- **Tropical Storm Ida (Nov 2009)** collapsed the lightweight funnel casing on the island, leaving a gap of about
  10 ft × 52 ft that divers now swim through (MBT Divers [TRADE]). FWC 2011 report: "minor structural change to the
  exterior covering of the smoke stack at 3.5 years ... following the tropical storm events". [FWC] (FWC dates the
  storms as 2007 and 2008, which conflicts with the dive shop's 2008/2009. **Treat the storm names as UNVERIFIED and
  say "storms" in the script.**)
- **Today (FWC page, current):** seabed 212 ft; flight deck ~145–150 ft; top of island 84 ft; gun platforms 107/109 ft;
  bridge 118 ft; 80 ft navigational clearance. FWC: "Due to past storms and settlement over time, the Oriskany Reef
  depths have changed." FWC says divers should **not enter the ship under any circumstances** because of
  corrosion and unknown structural damage. Removing items is illegal. [FWC]
- Dive-shop depth tables differ (top 65–84 ft; flight deck 140–146 ft; hangar 165–175 ft). Use FWC numbers.

## 11. Fish and divers: did it work?

- First year: about **4,200 chartered dive trips**, $2.2M in dive spending in Escambia + Baldwin counties, $3.6M total
  economic impact, 67 jobs (Haas Center, University of West Florida, 2007, quoted by UF/IFAS EDIS FE649 and NOAA).
  https://edis.ifas.ufl.edu/publication/FE649 ; https://repository.library.noaa.gov/view/noaa/41755/noaa_41755_DS1.pdf [GOV]
- EPA working paper (Morgan, Massey & Huth 2007; *Marine Resource Economics* 2009): use value $1,215 per diver per year,
  rising to $2,596 if a second ship were sunk beside her.
  https://www.epa.gov/sites/default/files/2014-12/documents/diving_demand_for_large_ship_artificial_reefs.pdf [EPA]
- Florida Senate analysis (2008) passes on a claim that Escambia County "recouped its $1 million investment in 3 days"
  and that dive trips were booked 12–16 months ahead (local officials' letter; **anecdotal, UNVERIFIED**).
- The pre-sinking hype: "$92 million a year" (Navy release, citing a 2004 FSU study; BBC). That number was **total
  artificial-reef spending in Escambia County**, not the Oriskany's own value. The 25 May 2006 local paper shows the
  1998 FSU/NOAA study gave $92M for *all* reefs and NOAA expected Oriskany to *add* about $9M a year.
  https://ufdcimages.uflib.ufl.edu/AA/00/06/97/61/00021/05-25-2006.pdf . **Good "hype vs reality" beat.**
- Marine life: red snapper, vermilion snapper, porgies, scamp, triggerfish (FWC sampling); dive press reports amberjack,
  barracuda, sharks, rays, mola, even whale sharks (TheTravel / dive operators, [TRADE], **UNVERIFIED** for rare species).
  Yellow cup coral covers the wreck (Divernet). Nickname: "Great Carrier Reef".

## 12. The reversal: PCBs in the fish

- The EPA permit **required** FWC + Escambia County to monitor PCBs in fish. [EPA]
- **FWC progress report (Dodrill, Mille, Horn & Turpin, 13 Apr 2011):** 8 sampling rounds, Dec 2006 – Nov 2010, 254
  legal-size fish from the Oriskany (184 red snapper) + 61 from a control reef. In the **first two years (rounds 1–4),
  mean total PCB in Oriskany fish was above both the Florida Dept of Health screening value (50 ppb) and the EPA screening
  value (20 ppb).** By round 5 (2.9 yr) it was below FDOH but slightly above EPA; by rounds 6–7 (3.5–3.9 yr) it was below
  both. "A declining trend." Red snapper, red porgy and whitebone porgy were highest; vermilion snapper and scamp never
  exceeded either value. Tagging showed red snapper tend to *stay* on the wreck.
  https://docslib.org/doc/2817262/progress-report-summarizing-the-reef-fish-sampling-pcb-analysis-results [FWC report, mirror]
- Center for Biological Diversity / Basel Action Network petition to EPA (Apr 2012), using FWC data: post-sinking red
  snapper mean **54 ppb vs 2.36 ppb pre-sinking**; all species mean 58.75 vs 3.8 ppb (a "1,446%" rise); 60 of 180
  post-sinking fish were above 20 ppb; Oriskany snapper averaged 55 ppb vs 7.6 ppb at a control reef in 2008. Levels
  were "more than twice" the EPA's pre-sinking forecast. [ADV, but it uses FWC numbers]
  https://www.biologicaldiversity.org/programs/oceans/pdfs/SINKEX_Petition_to_EPA_April_2012.pdf
- Same petition: the **Florida Dept of Health considered a snapper consumption advisory** for the site before the 1 Jun
  2010 snapper season, but did not issue one because the **Deepwater Horizon** federal fishing closure shut the area
  anyway. [ADV] (**UNVERIFIED: needs FDOH confirmation**)
- AP (Mercury News, 3 Mar 2012): fish "exceeded state and federal PCB standards in the first two years ... later
  collected saw a drop in those levels overall, but some still exceeded". The AP piece also says the finding "spurred
  Florida officials to bar further dumping along their coast". **UNVERIFIED and doubtful in that strong form**: Florida
  kept reefing ships (Vandenberg 2009). Reword before use. https://develop.mercurynews.com/2012/03/03/navy-practice-of-sinking-old-ships-raises-pollution-concerns
- Context: the pre-sinking UWF baseline found very low PCB in local fish
  (https://uwf.edu/media/university-of-west-florida/centers/environmental-diagnostics-and-bioremediation/docs/archive/Pre_reefing_Environmental_Assessment_oriskrpt2.pdf).
- **Aftermath:** Oriskany is still the only US Navy carrier reefed. The Navy turned to SINKEX target sinkings in deep
  water, and the 2012 petition attacked that too. (Later Navy policy on reefing large combatants: **not researched in this
  pass; open question**.)
