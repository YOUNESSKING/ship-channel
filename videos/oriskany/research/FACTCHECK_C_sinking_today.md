## E. 1-minute narration, sentence by sentence

1. **"Off the coast of Florida, engineers set off explosive charges inside an American aircraft carrier."**
   **CORRECTED.** They were a Navy Explosive Ordnance Disposal team / "Navy divers" (NAVREL, AP06, BBC06), not engineers, and the ship was decommissioned. Use: *"Off the coast of Florida, Navy demolition divers set off explosive charges inside a retired American aircraft carrier."*
2. **"Thirty-seven minutes later, she was gone."**
   **CONFIRMED.** 10:25 detonation → 11:02 under (MARAD07, NAVREL, AP06, GWR).
3. **"Nearly nine hundred feet of warship, now sitting two hundred and twelve feet down..."**
   **CONFIRMED.** 888 ft (Navy, AP, Guinness) is "nearly 900"; FWC's 911 ft overall is "over 900". Safer: *"About nine hundred feet of warship"*. 212 ft is the seafloor depth (FWC, NAVREL).
4. **"It took three years and about twenty million dollars just to get her ready to sink..."**
   **CONFIRMED.** PNJ06: "The Navy spent more than three years and $20 million preparing Oriskany to become an artificial reef"; AP06: "$20 million sinking"; NYT 2008: "spent $20 million to clean". Navy worked on it "since 2003" (NAVREL). Caveat for the long version: official totals differ (EPA BMP $15.63M stated / $23.6M line items, which include $4.9M for the scuttling itself; USNI $12.7M Navy spend), so "about twenty million" is the right level of precision.
5. **"...this month, a second famous American ship is scheduled to go down off the same coast, for the very same reason."**
   **CONFIRMED as of 4 Nov 2026.** SS United States, 12 Nov 2026, weather permitting, ~12.7 nmi ENE of Oriskany, as an artificial reef (OKA, USCG, MAREX26). "This month" is only true if published in November 2026; re-check after 12 Nov, and change to "this week" or past tense as needed.
