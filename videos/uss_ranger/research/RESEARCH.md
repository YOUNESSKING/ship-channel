# RESEARCH DOSSIER: USS Ranger (CV-61), "The Top Gun Carrier Sold for One Cent" (working title)

Compiled 2026-11-19. **Not yet through the 3-checker gate (CLAUDE.md).** Numbered claims with confidence live in `FACT_SHEET.md`
(source keys and URLs are the same there). This file is the narrative, the reasoning and the conflicts.
Rule used: official first, press second, Wikipedia only as a pointer. **Most official Navy sites failed this session** (see "Sources, ranked"),
so many "official" facts are read through reposts or veterans' sites and are capped at MED until a checker re-opens the originals.

## 0. Corrections to the brief (important for the script)

1. **Contract date.** NAVSEA announced the one-cent contract on **22 December 2014**. The tow left **5 March 2015**, arrived **12 July 2015**.
   Scrapping ended **1 November 2017**. There was no 2017 contract. Any script line "in 2017 the Navy sold her" is wrong.
2. **"Sold".** NAVSEA itself said: "This is not a sales contract, it is a procurement contract." The Navy paid the yard one cent to tow and break her up,
   and kept ownership until she was gone. "Sold for a penny" is the press headline; the video can use it and then correct it (a good reversal beat).
3. **Earlier carriers.** Forrestal (Oct 2013, All Star Metals) and Saratoga (May 2014, ESCO Marine) were one cent. **Constellation was $3 million** (June 2014).
   Independence (towed Mar to Jun 2017): price not found. So "the Navy has done this before" is true, but "every carrier cost one cent" is false.
4. **Top Gun.** The film's carrier was mainly **USS Enterprise (CVN-65)**. Ranger was used too (filming while she was docked at Coronado, mid-July 1985),
   but which shots are Ranger is not documented. Use "appeared in Top Gun", not "the Top Gun carrier". See section 4.
5. **Fire year.** One secondary snippet put the engine-room fire in 1995; it was **1 Nov 1983** (two sources).

## 2. The ship (short; the video holds the ending to the last act)

Ranger was the **third Forrestal-class carrier**: keel laid **2 Aug 1954** at Newport News, christened **29 Sep 1956**, commissioned **10 Aug 1957** at Norfolk Naval Shipyard
(A1-A4). After a Atlantic work-up she **rounded Cape Horn in mid-summer 1958** and became a Pacific carrier (A9, IBIB). The NAVSEA line that she spent her *entire* career in the Pacific
is an overclaim; her own history says otherwise. Size: about 56,000 tons light; length is 1,071 ft in one source and 1,050 ft in another, crew figures are loose (A6-A8). Do not put numbers on screen until NVR is read.

Service highlights worth a beat: 22 WestPac deployments (B2); Vietnam from the 1964-65 cruise (59 line days, "a record") through 1973 (B3-B5); the A-7A Corsair II introduced to combat in 1967-68 (B8);
the Arabian Sea in 1980-81 and 1987 (B13-B14); **Desert Storm** (8 Dec 1990 to 8 Jun 1991, B15); **Somalia/Restore Hope** (last cruise Aug 1992 to Jan 1993, B18).
The **1 Nov 1983 fire in Main Machinery Room 4** (fuel spill from an open valve in the Arabian Sea, 6 dead, 35 injured, B12) is the emotional hinge: the yard finished scrapping her exactly 34 years later and
held a moment of silence at 9:50 a.m. as the last section came out (I1-I2). Other incidents (1958 magazine explosion, 1965 fire, 1983 Wichita collision) are single-source (B9-B11).

## 3. The Navy's long goodbye (1993 to 2014)

- Decommissioned **10 Jul 1993** (D1). She was towed to Long Beach, then to Puget Sound in Sep 1994, into the reserve fleet in Oct 1994, and moored at Bremerton (D2-D3, D10). The Navy kept her as a mobilization asset for years (D4, EA snippet).
- **8 Mar 2004** removed from the Naval Vessel Register (D6, via NAVSEA statement; NVR itself unread). **22 Mar 2004** notice that she was available for donation (D7). **Eight years on donation hold** (D8).
- **Sep/Oct 2012:** the Navy lifted the hold and redesignated her for dismantling (D9). The Foundation fought it for two more years; NAVSEA's Dec 2014 release says the final no came "after eight years".
  *Conflict:* the NAVSEA repost says October 2014; ussranger.org (via MR) and UROH say 2012. Probably the decision was 2012 and the contract followed in 2014. A checker must read the EA.
- Why no museum: the USS Ranger Foundation (non-profit, 2001, Capt. Peter Ogle) wanted her at **Chinook Landing Marine Park, Fairview, Oregon**, on the Columbia River, in the Portland-Vancouver area (E1-E2).
  Navy rules required the recipient to show money for the first five years of operation (E8). The Foundation estimated about $10 million prep and up to $30 million all-in (E4); a press report says it raised about $100,000 against a $35 million plan (E5, single).
  NAVSEA said the Foundation could not raise the funds *and* could not overcome the difficulty of moving the ship up the Columbia to Fairview (E6). Draft/channel depth is **not** in any source read. A blog (relaying the Navy) names a **BNSF railroad bridge at river mile 80.9** (E7, unverified).
  Safest script claim: "the money did not come, and getting a carrier up the Columbia was a second problem the Navy said the group never solved."

## 4. The films (names only; we show no film imagery)

- **Top Gun (1986).** Production began 26 Jun 1985. Vintage Aviation News: filming "aboard USS Ranger and Enterprise" in the first six weeks. AFI (via summaries): five days at sea on Enterprise, 110 miles off San Diego; the Ranger docked near Coronado in mid-July 1985.
  The Enterprise media shop's behind-the-scenes video exists (TWZ). Which scenes are which: not documented. Forum and fan claims (Ranger = interiors/CIC, Carl Vinson also used) are unsourced.
  **Script wording:** "She appeared in Top Gun." Hook alternative: "the carrier that helped play the Top Gun carrier." Avoid "the Top Gun carrier".
- **Star Trek IV: The Voyage Home (1986).** The real Enterprise (CVN-65) was unavailable, so **Ranger stood in for her** (startrek.com). Navy extras; the name Ranger visible on some hats. A search summary of AFI says three days at NAS North Island (Uhura and Chekov scenes).
  Reactor secrecy as a reason is a fan-wiki claim: skip. A nice irony: a ship named Enterprise was played by Ranger.
- **Flight of the Intruder (1991).** AFI (via summary): the main carrier was **USS Independence** (10 days at sea). Ranger had only "an optional scene". The brief's "also filmed aboard" is MED/UNVERIFIED and minor. Fox lists it as a Ranger credit. Irony: Independence was later towed to the same Brownsville yard.
- The Navy's own statements never mention the films. They come only from press and film-side sources.

## 5. The penny

The contract, announced 22 Dec 2014: NAVSEA to International Shipbreaking Ltd. (Brownsville, an EMR subsidiary) for towing and dismantling, **$0.01** (F1-F2). The reasoning, in NAVSEA's words: the price "reflects the net price proposed ... which factored in the estimated proceeds from the sale of scrap metal" and is "the lowest price the Navy could possibly have paid" (F3-F4).
So the yard bid zero for the work and expects to pay for the tow and labour with what the steel and parts fetch; the one cent is a floor, not a sale price. No dollar scrap value or tow cost was published.
The vehicle was a five-year IDIQ contract for conventional carriers stricken from the NVR, competed among three yards (F8). The Navy keeps title until she is gone; the yard takes ownership of scrap as it is cut (F5-F6, same terms published for Constellation).
Over 56,000 tons of material were recycled (F10, an EMR figure). For contrast: Constellation was **$3 million** (G3); Kitty Hawk and John F. Kennedy went for one cent each in 2021 (G7). Nuclear Enterprise's disposal estimate runs in the billions (G8, unverified).
No official GAO/CRS number for conventional-carrier disposal was found: the "why a penny" explanation stays NAVSEA's own words.

## 6. The tow (the tow, not the scrapping, is the video's spectacle)

- Planned: leave Jan/Feb 2015, four to five months (H1). A Feb 15 start slipped over rigging (MR citing Kitsap Sun).
- **Left Bremerton Thursday 5 Mar 2015**, tugs through Port Orchard Bay (DVIDS), crowds at Manette (PDN15).
- Route: around South America because she did not fit the Panama Canal (H3). PDN15 says she headed "toward Cape Horn"; other sources say Strait of Magellan. Unresolved (H4). Distance about **16,000 miles** (H5, PDN15; same figure given for Independence in 2017).
- **Arrived Sunday 12 Jul 2015** (AP): 129 days. Spectators lined the jetties at South Padre Island (H6-H7). Brownsville's second former warship that year; Constellation had come in January (G5).
- Tug: **Lindsey Foss** at departure (DVIDS); a pre-departure report named Crosby Tugs. Whole-voyage tug unverified (H8).
- A veterans' farewell voyage in the Brownsville ship channel was reported 24-25 Jul 2015 (MR, secondary).

## 7. The end

Dismantling took **just over two years** and ended **1 Nov 2017**, 34 years to the day after the MMR4 fire; a moment of silence at 9:50 a.m. for the six sailors (I1-I2, EMR release carried by two outlets).
What survived: **over five tons of historic items** to the USS Lexington Museum, Corpus Christi (I3: contents not listed); gauges, windows and controls from MMR4 to the USS Ranger Association (Oct 2017) for an exhibit on the six dead (I4); dog tags minted from her brass and copper and cut flight-deck sections sold on eBay (I5).
**No bell, anchor or plaque location was found** (I7): ask the Association and the Lexington. This is the only "where is it now" answer the video can give, and it should be flagged as incomplete.

## 8. Surprising angles for the hook and 30-second wows (each with its source)

1. **The Navy paid one cent, and it was the lowest the Navy could legally pay.** NAVSEA 22 Dec 2014 (F2, F4).
2. **It was not a sale.** "This is a procurement contract": the Navy owned her until she was gone (F5).
3. **A 56,000-ton carrier for the price of a penny, because the scrap pays the bill.** NAVSEA net-price wording (F3, F10).
4. **Her sister Constellation cost the Navy $3 million; Ranger cost one cent** (G3 vs F2): a bidding twist, same yard.
5. **Star Trek's Enterprise was Ranger**: the film's carrier was played by a different carrier, with her name on sailors' hats (C4).
6. **The real "Enterprise" was too far away; the stand-in was a carrier with a different name** (STARTREK). Use as a wow for the film beat, no imagery.
7. **A Top Gun carrier that wasn't the Top Gun carrier**: Enterprise sailed for the film; Ranger sat at the pier in Coronado (C1-C2). Reversal beat.
8. **She was on a donation list for eight years and nobody could pay**: 2004 to 2012 (D8).
9. **A Columbia River museum, a railroad bridge and no money**: BNSF bridge at mile 80.9 (E7, unverified) plus the Navy's five-year-operations rule (E8).
10. **16,000 miles under tow, 129 days** (H5, H7): her last voyage was 4 months of towing, and the Navy forecast 4-5.
11. **She rounded Cape Horn in 1958 to start her Pacific life, and rounded it again under tow in 2015 to die in the Gulf** (A9, H4 conflict: confirm route first).
12. **The last piece came out on the anniversary of the fire that killed six**: 1 Nov 2017, 34 years to the day, silence at 9:50 a.m. (I2). Best closing beat.
13. **The same yard that broke Ranger had cut Constellation, then Independence**: Independence was Flight of the Intruder's carrier (G6, C5).
14. **Her brass became dog tags and her deck went on eBay** (I5).
15. **Last trap 11 Mar 1993, an F-14A, the 330,683rd landing** (B19, UNVERIFIED: needs a second source) and **Desert Storm: 2,114 tons of ordnance** (B17, UNVERIFIED).

- **Length 1,071 ft / crew 5,600 / 88,000 tons** until NVR is read (A7-A8).
- **Private photos and drone clips** (gCaptain/War History drone video, veterans' photos, Foundation images): rights unknown. Use NHHC/NARA/DVIDS public-domain images only; log each in `CREDITS.md`.

- Cause/blame for the 1983 fire beyond "a fuel spill from an open valve" (MAREX17) and the disciplinary summary: single source.
