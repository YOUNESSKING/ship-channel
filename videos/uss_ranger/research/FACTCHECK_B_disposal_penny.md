# FACT-CHECK B: USS Ranger (CV-61) disposal, the penny, the museum campaign (FACT_SHEET D, E, F, G, J)

Checker B (independent). Checked 19 Nov 2026. **Limit up front: every navsea.navy.mil, defense.gov, nvr.navy.mil, history.navy.mil,
globalsecurity.org (paywall, HTTP 402) and web.archive.org URL was blocked (403 / TLS error / refused) from this environment.** The Navy
text below is therefore read from verbatim reprints (Marine Log via recyclingships.blogspot, Navy Times, MarineLink, WorkBoat, the
Foundation's own letter), plus search-engine extracts of the NAVSEA Q&A PDF. No row below rests on a Wikipedia page. No DoD contracts
announcement for 22 Dec 2014 was found: the award was a NAVSEA news release, not a defense.gov contract line, as far as these searches show.
No GAO/CRS report on conventional-carrier disposal was found.

## Headline findings (script-safe wording)

1. **"Sold for one cent" is press shorthand; the Navy says it is not a sale. CONFIRMED.** NAVSEA, 22 Dec 2014: "This is not a sales
   contract, it is a procurement contract." The Navy pays the contractor $0.01 to tow and dismantle. **Script: "The Navy didn't sell her.
   It hired a shipbreaker, and paid it one cent."**
2. **Why a penny. CONFIRMED.** Price "reflects the net price proposed by International Shipbreaking" after it "considered the estimated
   proceeds from the sale of the scrap metal to be generated from dismantling". "$0.01 is the lowest price the Navy could possibly have
   paid the contractor." So the bid was a net of tow cost against scrap value, floored at a cent. The Navy never published the gross
   figures. **Script: "Her steel was worth about as much as the tow and the cutting cost, so the bid came to one cent, the lowest the Navy
   could pay."** Do not state the scrap value.
3. **Who owned the ship in the tow: NOT stated in any text read. UNVERIFIED as a legal point.** The Navy's wording ("procurement")
   implies it hired a contractor; the sheet's "Navy keeps the ship until she is gone" comes from 19FortyFive commentary and Navy Times
   ("the Navy owned the ship and contracted ISL"), not a Navy text. **Script: "The Navy hired ISL to tow and scrap her." Avoid "the
   shipbreaker owned her" and "the Navy owned her until the last piece".**
4. **Date of the Navy's "no donation" decision: the FACT_SHEET conflict is resolved to 2012.** Navy Q&A (extract): removed from donation
   hold **26 Sep 2012**, approved by the ASN(RDA). Oregonlive 4 Oct 2012 reported it; the Foundation's own letter (blog dated 21 Dec 2012)
   says the Navy decided "not to proceed with the donation". The Dec 2014 release's "October decision" is a 2012 decision restated
   ambiguously (it reads as 2014 on its face; the Navy said in Jan 2015 it would not return the ship to donation hold). **Script: "In
   2012 the Navy ended the donation hold."** Do not say "October 2014".
5. **Why the Navy refused.** Navy 2014 release: after "eight years on donation hold" the Foundation "was unable to raise the necessary
   funds" and could not overcome "the physical obstacles of transporting her up the Columbia River to Fairview, Oregon." Navy Q&A
   (extract): only **$105,000 evidenced of ~$31.85 million** start-up cost; 31 Jul 2012 revised application showed no fundraising progress
   and "no solution to the Columbia River bridge obstructions". Chris Johnson (2012): "no organization has been able to meet the Navy's
   minimum requirements." **Script-safe: "raised about a hundred thousand dollars toward a bill of roughly thirty million, and nobody had
   solved the bridge on the river."**
6. **Foundation money. CORRECTED.** Foundation said "more than $600,000" raised (Oregonlive 2012, via blog). Navy counted $105,000 as
   **evidenced**. Fox/HRANA "$100,000 of $35 million" is a rounding of the Navy figure with the wrong budget. **Use "the Navy counted about
   $105,000" and attribute to the Navy.** Do not say "raised only $100,000" as fact.
7. **Other carriers.** Forrestal and Saratoga one cent: CONFIRMED. **Constellation was not one cent: $3 million**, CONFIRMED (MarineLink).
   **Independence: not one cent. About $6 million paid to ISL (CORRECTED/UNVERIFIED single source: ISL president quoted in local press).**
   Kitty Hawk and JFK one cent each: CONFIRMED (TWZ, Oct 2021). **Script: "Several of the last big carriers went for a cent. Not all."**
8. **Decommissioning 10 Jul 1993: CONFIRMED. Place: UNVERIFIED.** A navsource ceremony program reads San Diego; sheet's North Island is
   plausible, Bremerton ceremony is wrong. Stricken **8 Mar 2004: CONFIRMED** (Navy text via Marine Log). **Script: "decommissioned in
   July 1993" and no place.**
