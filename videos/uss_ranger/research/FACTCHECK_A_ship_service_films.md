## Headline findings (script-safe wording)
1. **Top Gun: "Ranger appeared in Top Gun" is TRUE and safe; "the Top Gun carrier" is not.** Enterprise (CVN-65) was the main at-sea carrier.
   Two independent-enough sources put Ranger in the production: ASC magazine (via search summary: "shot aboard aircraft carriers U.S.S. Ranger and U.S.S. Enterprise") and Vintage Aviation News ("those aboard USS Ranger and Enterprise" during the six weeks after 26 Jun 1985); AFI (snippet): mid-Jul 1985 filming aboard the docked Ranger near Coronado. Scene split is **not documented in any primary source**. DAHP says Ranger "served as the set for the interiors" including "the memorable opening scenes set in the radar room and later scenes in the pilots' ready room" (cites only the DVD). Fan guides say interiors; one forum post says the CAG chew-out scene was in the in-port admiral's cabin. Wording: **"Ranger's interiors were used for Top Gun"** is MED; **"the radar-room opening" and "ready room" are UNVERIFIED specifics**: do not caption specific scenes. Safe line: "Hollywood filmed aboard her for Top Gun, mostly inside the ship, while the flight-deck action was shot on Enterprise."
2. **Star Trek IV: CONFIRMED (2 sources).** startrek.com: "The U.S.S. Enterprise CV-65 was actually unavailable for filming. The USS Ranger CV-61 stood in for the Enterprise." DAHP also: Ranger "stood in for the USS Enterprise (the 'nuclear wessel')". AFI (snippet): three days at NAS North Island, scenes with Uhura and Chekov. Ranger caps visible on extras. Safe: "In 1986 she played the Enterprise (the aircraft carrier, CVN-65) in Star Trek IV." Do not say the reason was classification (secondary only).
3. **Flight of the Intruder (1991): Ranger is NOT the main carrier.** AFI (snippet, two catalog entries): main shoot aboard **USS Independence** (CV-62), ten days at sea off California (IMDb trivia: Nov 1989), 110-person crew. AFI also notes Milius shot "an optional scene" with Danny Glover and Brad Johnson aboard Ranger. DAHP says "Ranger ... appearing in Flight of the Intruder" (no detail). Safe: "She also had a small part in Flight of the Intruder." Do not use Ranger footage as that film's carrier.
4. **Angled deck "first from the keel": UNVERIFIED as a flat superlative; usable with attribution.** NavSource says first US carrier designed and built with an angled deck from the keel up; Wikipedia (citing GlobalSecurity) says the same and that Forrestal and Saratoga were converted to angled decks during construction; DAHP says "first U.S. carrier to be designed and built with an angled deck" (cites Friedman 1983). But the second claim (Forrestal/Saratoga) is itself uncited and I found **no official NHHC/NAVSEA text**. Forrestal and Saratoga had angled decks at commissioning, so "first angled-deck carrier" is false; the nuance is purely design/laying-down. Script: "among the first built with an angled deck" or skip. Do not say "first".
5. **"Top Gun of the Pacific" nickname: CONFIRMED as a nickname, not as an official title.** DAHP: Nicknamed "Top Gun of the Pacific"; NavSource page: "Top Gun" was the ship's nickname, referenced in a 1982 flight-deck formation for the 25th anniversary (pre-dates the 1986 film). Wikipedia infobox "Top Gun". Safe: "her crew called her the Top Gun of the Pacific, years before the movie". Strong irony hook. Do not call it official.
6. **Specs: length is 1,067 ft per DAHP (Foundation, from the Navy's own docs); 1,071 ft (Wikipedia, CAMAS10), 1,050 (PDN15), 1,039 overall? (NavSource, probably a dated DANFS-style figure) all circulate.** Use "more than a thousand feet / about 1,070 feet". Displacement: 56,300 tons standard/light (NHHC ibiblio "56,300-ton"), ~81,000-82,000 full load (Wikipedia 81,101 long tons; DAHP 82,000 tons). Script: "56,000 tons empty, more than 80,000 tons loaded". Crew: DAHP "nearly 6,000" capacity; Wikipedia complement 3,826 ship; NavSource crew 3,800 to 4,280; air wing about 2,000 on top is unverified. Script: "about 5,000 to 5,500 with the air wing" is NOT verified. Safe: "thousands: roughly 3,800 sailors plus an air wing".
7. **WestPac count: CONFLICT.** 22 (NAVSEA 2014 via press, uss-ranger.org) vs "some twenty" (NHHC ibiblio) vs "24 major deployments totaling over 4,500 days" (DAHP citing navy.mil "Carrier Deployments by Year"). Script: **"more than twenty deployments"**.
8. **Vietnam: say "from 1964 to 1973" and "13 campaign stars".** DAHP: "Ranger's service in Vietnam earned the ship 13 battle stars, the most of any carrier since World War II" (secondary; also in Fox, CAMAS10). The uss-ranger.org awards page lists 18 Vietnam campaigns of which Ranger was deployed in 13 (a count of campaigns, not an official star total). NavSource lists 11 Vietnam Service Medal stars: a conflict. NHHC says "returned to the war zone six times between late 1965 and early 1973" (after the fifth WestPac, 1965). DAHP lists seven Vietnam-era cruises 1964-73. Wording: "seven combat cruises, 1964 to 1973" is supportable (counting the 1964-65 cruise); "13 battle stars" only as "credited with 13 stars", MED.
9. **1983 fire: CONFIRMED. 1 Nov 1983, six dead, 35 injured, North Arabian Sea.** UPI same day: "six crewmen killed, at least 35 injured", first fire 9:50 am local in one of four main machinery spaces and an adjoining auxiliary space, out within an hour, reflash out by 12:07 pm; most injuries smoke, burns, heat exhaustion; NavSource: fuel and oil fire in an engine room, six killed and 35 injured. Cause (fuel transfer, open valve): only Maritime Executive 2017. UPI says two fires. Script: "a fuel fire in a main machinery room killed six sailors". Name MMR4 only with the Maritime Executive/Wikipedia pair; do not say "34 years to the day" without the scrapping-date source check (that is a different checker's row).
10. **Desert Storm: dates CONFIRMED, numbers partly.** Left San Diego **8 Dec 1990**, reached the Persian Gulf launch position **15 Jan 1991**, first combat ops **17 Jan 1991**, returned San Diego **8 Jun 1991** (NavSource; DAHP; uss-ranger.org). First pre-dawn 16-aircraft strike over Iraq 17 Jan (Defense Media Network); one A-6E lost on the third night (DMN; Wikipedia says 18 Jan, crew killed). **Sorties: "more than 4,250" (DAHP, citing Foundation magazine) / "more than 4,300 combat sorties" (Defense Media Network). Not 4,253 as in the fact sheet.** Script: "over 4,000 sorties". Ordnance 4.3 million lb: one advocacy source only; "more than any other carrier" is UNVERIFIED (GAO per-aircraft rates: Midway 1.59, Ranger 1.41, Roosevelt 1.36). Ranger flew mostly night missions. "Only West Coast carrier": DAHP also says so, but still one chain; use "the Pacific Fleet's carrier from San Diego".
11. **Somalia: she took part, but dates differ.** uss-ranger.org awards: Restore Hope 7-15 Dec 1992 (the AFEM window). DAHP: Bush diverted her from the Gulf in Dec 1992, about 60 planes, air wing flew reconnaissance, airborne traffic control, logistics and on-call close air support for Navy/Marine amphibious forces; 63 digital photos sent by satellite (first from a ship at sea). Wikipedia: left the Gulf 4 Dec 1992. Photo caption: A-6E over Somalia 7 Dec 1992. The fact sheet's "19 Dec 1992 landing" is not mine to confirm (the Marine landing at Mogadishu was 9 Dec 1992, a known date; check that row). Safe: "in December 1992 she flew in support of Operation Restore Hope".
12. **Hull and dates:** keel 2 Aug 1954, launched/christened 29 Sep 1956 (sponsor Mrs. Arthur W. Radford, NavSource), commissioned 10 Aug 1957, Norfolk Naval Shipyard, Capt. Charles D. Booth II: three sources agree (DAHP, NavSource, Wikipedia). All carry the same chain back to DANFS, which I could not open. HIGH enough to script. "Third Forrestal-class" CONFIRMED (DAHP, NHHC). Only Forrestal-class carrier with no SLEP: CONFIRMED by two (NHHC ibiblio "only one of her four-ship class not to receive the 1980s SLEP"; DAHP).
