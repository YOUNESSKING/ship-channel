# FACT-CHECK A: the 1917–1922 wooden steamship program (USSB / Emergency Fleet Corporation)

Checker A (independent). Checked 15 Nov 2026 against primary/official sources: the US Shipping Board's own annual
reports (1st–7th, 1917–1923, full text on archive.org; 1923 also cross-read on the FMC's own PDF), Admiral Sims's
1920 memoir (U-boat losses), and a 1922 newspaper. Wikipedia was not used. Scope: FACT_SHEET section C (all),
D1–D8, F1–F2, and the program parts of the "Conflicts summary".

**Headline findings**
1. **Sale price is $750,000, not $862,000.** Newspapers at the time give $750,000. The Board's own table puts the
   233 ships plus two small tug lots at $813,380.06 in total, which rules out $862,000 for the ships alone. Date:
   **auction at Washington, 12 Sept 1922**.
2. **Official build totals: 304 wooden + 18 composite cargo steamers = 322.** The total of 322 matches the NRHP;
   the NRHP's split (296 + 26) does not match the Board's tables.
3. **"134 completed by October 1918" is not supported.** The Board's own count is **84 wooden + 8 composite
   delivered by 31 Oct 1918** (227 wooden hulls launched by then).
4. **"Short cruising range" (C15) is now CONFIRMED** from an official source: the Board rated the Ferris type at
   about **3,000 miles steaming radius**.
5. **Resale-value collapse:** the official sale averages are about $209,000 a ship (1919–20), then 21 ships for
   $120,423 in total (about $5,700 each, 1921–22), then about $3,200 each (Sept 1922). The NRHP's
   "$650,000 → $100,000" could not be confirmed.
6. **Program cost:** the Board's ledger shows **$274.1 million** spent on wood and composite vessels by 30 June 1920.
   This is the best official figure for "what it cost". It replaces the "unverified" C11.

## Wording to avoid
- "$862,000", "234 ships", "134 ships finished by October 1918", "322 ships by the end of the war",
  "296 wooden and 26 composite" (use 322 total), "40 shipyards in 17 states" as the full count,
  "$650,000 → $100,000", "$50,000 a month" stated as fact, "the world's fastest ship ever built" for *Aberdeen*,
  any quotation marks around Denman's "faster than the Germans could sink them", "they never sailed".

## Not checked / out of scope
Fires, Mallows Bay burns, Bethlehem salvage, the 1925 burn and later history (sections D9 onward, E) belong to the
other checkers. One side note for them: SSC (citing the builder's material) says *Aberdeen*'s stripped hull burned at
Widewater in **April 1923**. FACT_SHEET D12 gives her first test burn as **21 Sep 1923**. Check this.
