## A. Pearl Harbor, 7 December 1941

**The berth.** Nevada lay at **berth F-8** (CPAC42), "moored singly off Ford Island" (DANFS63), just astern of Arizona
(NPS-BSR: "moored just behind the USS Arizona"). The other battleships were in pairs or in dock; being alone at the end
of the row gave her room to move (DANFS63). NHHC-PH1 adds that she was the "eldest (by a few months)" of the battleships
present.

**The torpedo.** AR41 (the ship's own report) is clear: attack first seen **0801**; at **0802** her machine guns opened
on torpedo planes on the port beam; "One plane dropped a torpedo which struck the Nevada on the port bow." The damage
section places the hit at about frame 42, even with the forward edge of No. 2 barbette. NHHC-PH1: "hit by one torpedo
during the last part of the Japanese torpedo planes' attack", below the two forward turrets; her blister protection
"resisted the warhead's explosion fairly well" but the inner bulkhead leaked. NHHC drydock photos (NH 84003, NH 64306,
19 Feb 1942) show the Type 91 hole between about frames 37/38 and 44/46. PW's "one or possibly two torpedoes" has no
support in the primaries. **One torpedo.**

**The bombs: the numbers disagree.**
- AR41 summary: "the Nevada suffered **at least six (6) bomb hits** and one torpedo hit. It is possible that as many as
  ten bomb hits may have been received." The report walks through the hits: two on the forecastle at frame 15, one at
  frame 25, a possible very large one near frame 27 forward of No. 1 turret, one through the bridge into casemate 6
  against the stack, one on the boat deck near frame 80 (galley).
- DANFS63: "struck by one torpedo and two, possibly three, bombs" before she got under way, then "struck again".
- PHAM (citing the BuShips war damage report): five dive-bomb hits in the second wave.
- **Script wording:** "at least five bombs" or "half a dozen bombs". Do not give a single exact count.

**Who was in command.** The captain (F. W. Scanland, who signed AR41) was ashore; PHAM (from Walter Lord) says the
executive officer was too. AR41: "Lieutenant Commander **Francis J. Thomas**, U.S. Naval Reserve, the Commanding Officer
during the greater part of the attack ... got the ship underway within forty minutes and headed down channel."
Thomas "correctly decided that it was urgently necessary to get underway to avoid destruction of the ship due to the
proximity of the Arizona which was surrounded with burning oil and afire from stem to stern." Lt Lawrence E. Ruff acted
as navigator. Chief Quartermaster R. Sedberry "ordered the engine room to prepare to get underway before the arrival of
officers on the bridge" (AR41). PHAM (Lord) adds that Ensign Taussig had two boilers lit instead of one that morning,
which is why steam was available: secondary, MED at best.

**Getting under way.** "Before getting underway at **0840**" (AR41). CPAC42: "At 0840, the Nevada, without assistance,
cleared berth F-8 and proceeded down the South Channel." NHHC-OL: "The only battleship able to get underway during the
7 December 1941 Pearl Harbor Raid." NHHC-PH1: under way "about a half-hour after she was torpedoed", which matches
0802 + ~38 min.

**Two groundings, not one.** Popular retellings say "beached at Hospital Point". The record shows two steps:
1. CPAC42: "at about **0910**, and while attempting to negotiate the turn in the channel, Nevada temporarily grounded"
   near floating drydock YFD-2, i.e. off Hospital Point on the Navy Yard side. NHHC-PH1 says she "was run aground on the
   Navy Yard side of the channel, just south of Ford Island" and then "twisted around until she was facing back up the
   harbor".
2. CPAC42: "At **1035**, the Nevada, then aground near YFD-2, got clear and at **1045** voluntarily grounded on Waipio
   Peninsula opposite Hospital Point." NHHC-PH1: "With the help of tugs, Nevada then backed across the way and grounded,
   stern-first, on the other side of the channel." Photo 80-G-33020 caption: "aground and burning off Waipio Point".
**Why:** DANFS63: "Fearing she might sink in the channel, blocking it, she was beached at Hospital Point." NPS-BSR says
the same. By the next day she had settled to the bottom in shallow water (NHHC-PH1, NHHC-SALV). Her poor watertight
integrity, after decades of modification, let the water spread (NHHC-PH1, NHHC-SALV).

**Heroes (Medal of Honor).**
- **Chief Boatswain Edwin J. Hill** (AR41 and his citation in NHHC-HILL): "led his men of the line-handling details ...
  to the quays, cast off the lines and swam back to his ship. Later, while on the forecastle attempting to let go the
  anchors, he was blown overboard and killed by the explosion of several bombs." HIGH.
- **Donald K. Ross** (NHHC-ROSS citation): kept the forward dynamo room going alone "until blinded and unconscious",
  was revived, then did the same in the after dynamo room. On 7 Dec he was a Warrant Officer Machinist; he became Chief
  Machinist in March 1942; the citation prints his later rank. **AR41 does not mention Ross** (it was written 8 days
  after), so his story rests on the citation: MED, but the citation is an official document.
- AR41 also praises Ensign J. K. Taussig Jr. (kept directing AA fire badly wounded, lowered by stretcher), Ensign T. H.
  Taylor, BM1 A. Solar (killed), S1c W. F. Neundorf Jr. (killed).

**Casualties: conflicting.**
| Source | Killed | Wounded |
|---|---|---|
| NHHC-PH1 | "fifty officers and men were killed in action" (crew "nearly 1500") | — |
| DANFS63 (1963) | 60 | 109 |
| PW | 50 | 109 |
| Nevada Appeal (press, via search) | 50 | 105 |
The gap may be men who died of wounds later, but no source read says so. **UNVERIFIED** either way. Safe script: "about
fifty men were killed" (NHHC) or avoid the number.

**The band and the colours: treat as oral history.** The story (MILBAND, Walter Lord's *Day of Infamy* via secondaries):
bandleader Oden McMillan's 23-man band formed aft for 0800 colours, began the anthem as the first planes came in, a plane
strafed the fantail and hit the flag, and the band finished the anthem before scattering. AR41 says only that the 5-inch
AA battery "was partially manned for routine daily 0800 battery and fire control check". **No NHHC/NPS document read
confirms the band detail, the shredded flag or the exact timing.** Versions differ (some put the first bomb mid-anthem).
Mark **UNVERIFIED**; if used, say "survivors remembered..." and do not show a "torn flag" image as proof.

**Salvage and repair.** Two salvors died of hydrogen sulphide gas on 7 Feb 1942; refloated **12 Feb 1942**; Drydock No.
2 on 18 Feb; out 15 Mar; sailed for Puget Sound under her own power **22 Apr 1942**; back in the fleet late 1942;
salvage took >400 dives and >1,500 diver-hours (all NHHC-SALV; refloating date also DANFS63). HIGH.

## B. Return to the war

- **Attu**, 11–18 May 1943 (DANFS63; NHHC-OL "Attu landings in May 1943"). Brief.
- **Normandy.** Supported the Utah Beach landings on 6 June 1944 (NHHC-OL caption to 80-G-231961, "photographed from
  USS Quincy"); in action 6–17 June and again on 25 June (DANFS63). DANFS63: her guns "ranged as far as 17 miles inland
  ... Shore batteries straddled her 27 times, but failed to diminish her accurate fire." The NHHC Operation Neptune page,
  seen only as a search snippet, gives 337 rounds of 14-inch and about 2,700 of 5-inch: **UNVERIFIED** until opened.
- **"Only Pearl Harbor battleship at Normandy."** The US battleships off Normandy were Nevada, Texas and Arkansas (the
  British ones were not at Pearl Harbor). Texas and Arkansas were Atlantic Fleet ships and not among the battleships at
  Pearl Harbor (DANFS63 "the other 8 battleships"; NPS-BSR list). So Nevada was the only battleship present at both.
  HIGH as a derived fact. **Do not use NPS-BSR's wording "the only ship to have fought in both the attack on Pearl Harbor
  and the Normandy landings" or "flagship for the D-Day invasion"**: neither is supported (other small ships may have
  been at both; Adm. Deyo's bombardment flagship is usually given as Tuscaloosa, and one secondary says Nevada:
  UNVERIFIED).
- **Cherbourg**, 25 June 1944 (DANFS63). The brief's "hit-and-miss by shore batteries" detail is not verified beyond
  DANFS63's "straddled 27 times" (which covers 6–25 June).
- **Southern France** (Dragoon), 15 Aug–25 Sep 1944, dueling the Toulon batteries armed with 13.4-inch guns from
  scuttled French battleships (DANFS63). Guns relined at New York afterwards.
- **Iwo Jima**, 16 Feb–7 Mar 1945 (DANFS63; photo 80-G-K-3510, 19 Feb 1945).
- **Okinawa.** Kamikaze hit **27 Mar 1945**: 11 killed and a main-battery turret (No. 3) damaged (DANFS63, NHHC-OL,
  JMA22; JMA22 adds 49 wounded). Shore battery hit **5 Apr 1945**: 2 killed (DANFS63, JMA22). Off Okinawa until 30 June.
- **Battle stars: 7** (DANFS63; other pages copy it). MED until the modern DANFS is checked.
- **Arizona's guns.** JMA22: after Pearl Harbor three of Arizona's 14-inch guns from her Turret 2 (serials 19L4, 18L3,
  17L3) were refurbished and mounted in Nevada's Turret 1; one Oklahoma gun (76L3) went into Turret 3 in 1945; "All of
  these guns went down with the ship." NHHC's commemoration page (snippet only) says two Oklahoma guns went into Turret 3.
  MED; a strong emotional beat ("she carried Arizona's guns back into the war, and to the bottom").

## C. Operation Crossroads, Bikini Atoll, 1946

**Why Nevada.** JMA22 (citing Shurcliff): chosen as aiming point for the air-dropped test as "the most rugged ship
available". BAB47 calls her the "Zeropoint ship" and the "bull's-eye battleship".

**The paint.** The BuShips Able report (BS-A1) itself says "international orange": "Where white paint adjoined
international orange, the orange burned. Where black adjoined orange, the black burned and the orange remained." JMA22:
"international orange, the bright color used on San Francisco's Golden Gate Bridge", decks trimmed white; known at Bikini
as a "big, red lobster" and "the ship with scarlet fever" (Shurcliff 1947; Weisgall 1994). BAB47: a flashing lamp on
Nevada and her "high-visibility paint" guided the bombardier. **Use "international orange" or "bright orange"; avoid
"red" (PW) except in the nickname quote.**

**Crew and preparation.** Crew of **403** (DNA82-2), arrived Bikini 28 May 1946, left the ship on 30 June for USS George
Clymer (JMA22 citing DNA 6032F p. 364; DNA82-2). They watched Able from 21 miles and Baker from 16 miles (DNA82-2).
Nevada carried test gear on deck (Army tanks, guns, trucks: JMA22 table from the BuOrd report) and two OS2U-3
Kingfisher floatplanes (BUAER).

**Test Able, 1 July 1946.** DNA82-2: 1 July, 0900, 23 kt, air burst ~520 ft. BAB47: burst "at exactly 34 seconds after
9:00 a.m." local; B-29 **Dave's Dream**, aircraft 44-27354, took off from Kwajalein at 5:55 a.m. Yield: 23 kt (DNA,
IOM96) vs ~20 kt (NPS37, JMA22, older figure): use 23 kt (DTRA's figure). The bomb name **"Gilda"** (and the Rita
Hayworth picture) appears only in secondary sources read here: **UNVERIFIED**; say "nicknamed Gilda, according to the
crews' stories" only if a checker finds a primary.

**How badly it missed: the figures.**
| Figure | What it measures | Source |
|---|---|---|
| "1500 to 2000 ft. to the west of the intended bull's-eye ship NEVADA" | aim point to burst, plan view | BAB47 (official, 1947) |
| "several hundred yards west of a point directly above the target ship NEVADA" | same | BAB47 (Joint Chiefs evaluation board text) |
| 710 yards | "missed the intended target ship, USS Nevada, by 710 yards" | NPS37 |
| 2,130 ft (649 m) | burst to battleship | JMA22 |
| "about 1750 feet off target" | DNA 1984 fact sheet | IOM96 |
| 980 ft short, 1,870 ft left (≈2,110 ft) | bomb fall error | NWA (secondary) |
| 600 yd, bearing 105°T | ship's distance from centre of burst | BUAER table |
| 615 yd | ship's distance (damage list) | NPS37 |
| 639–811 yd | slant range (ship is 583 ft long) | DNA82-1 |
All agree on roughly 600–710 yards (1,750–2,130 ft). The spread is mostly what is measured (aim point vs nearest part of
a 583-ft ship vs slant range). **Script: "missed by roughly 700 yards", "by more than a third of a mile".** BAB47 adds
that the cause "has been the subject of long study; but no answer has been found". The burst came almost over the
transport Gilliam (50 yd per NPS37; 150 yd per BUAER).

**What Able sank.** Five ships: Gilliam and Carlisle (both quickly), destroyers Lamson and Anderson (1 July), Japanese
cruiser Sakawa (2 July) (NPS37 App. I; BAB47 "five mortally-wounded ships"; IOM96 "sinking five ships"). HIGH.

**What Able did to Nevada** (BS-A1). Both topmasts down; superstructure "mildly dished"; main deck aft depressed up to
16½ inches; **all boilers out of commission** (casings failed); whistle and siren demolished; fires in Army quartermaster
stores on deck burned through the wooden deck and cables; a canvas windbreaker ignited by heat; paint scorched through
one coat; hull above the waterline intact, only seepage into four voids near the skeg; no change in draft or list.
BAB47: "escaped with moderate damage ... her hull was practically undamaged". Support-ship logs in DNA82-1 record a
fireboat "fighting fire on USS Nevada" after the test. JMA22: the blast "killed the ship's electrical systems, rendering
Nevada 'dead'".

**The animals aboard Nevada.** BS-A1: "No damage to pilot house (**goats and rats in pilot house apparently unaffected
by heat and pressure**, no report as to radiological effects)." Crossroads as a whole carried 204 goats, 200 pigs, ~5,000
rats, 60 guinea pigs, 200 mice (BAB47); Test B used only pigs and rats (BAB47). Whether animals were aboard Nevada for
Baker is not stated in what was read.

**Test Baker, 25 July 1946.** DNA82-2: 0835, 23 kt, ~90 ft under water. BAB47: 8:34:59.7 a.m. local; bomb hung under
LSM-60 (NWA, NPS37). **Nevada's distance: 1,060 yards, bearing 000°T** (BUAER table, primary); JMA22 rounds to 1,000
yards. For comparison (BUAER): Arkansas 240 yd, Saratoga 380 yd, New York 940 yd, Pennsylvania 1,210 yd.
Sunk by Baker: Arkansas, Saratoga, submarines Apogon and Pilotfish, LSM-60 on 25 July, Nagato on 29 July (NPS37 App. I);
also the dry dock ARDC-13 and barge YO-160 (NWA). Nevada (BS-B): "not sufficient to sink the ship due to the
distance"; underwater shock loosened foundations and opened seams; steering gear and after diesel-generator rooms
flooded; list went from ½° to 1¼° port; boiler casings (repaired after Able) ruptured again. JMA22 quoting the BuShips
report: "the whole topside ... covered by radioactive spray and water containing sand and coral which was highly
radioactive." **Survived, but poisoned.**

**Scale.** BAB47: "42,000 men, 242 ships, 156 airplanes ... 204 goats, 200 pigs, 5000 rats". IOM96: "approximately
42,000 men", "more than 90 vessels" in the target fleet. DNA82-1: 95 target vessels identified overall, 88 per shot,
84 tracked in the dose study; Navy personnel 39,418. BAB47 (JCS board): "90 targets anchored in the lagoon" at Able.
**Script: "about ninety target ships", "42,000 men".**

## D. The end, 1946–1948

- **Decommissioned 29 August 1946** (DNA82-2; DANFS63; NHHC-OL "August 1946"). **Where:** DNA82-2 says "at Kwajalein",
  then "towed to Pearl Harbor for radiological testing, arriving 15 May 1947". DANFS63's "returned to Pearl Harbor to
  decommission 29 August" compresses this. JMA22: towed first to Kwajalein, then to Pearl Harbor. Prefer DNA82-2.
- **"Too radioactive to scrap"**: widely repeated, but no document read states the reason for disposal. NHHC-OL:
  "damaged and radioactive", "too old for retention". Safe: "too damaged and too radioactive to keep".
- **The sinking (JMA22, from the ships' logs and 1948 press; DNA82-2; DANFS63):**
  - 26 July 1948: tug Jicarilla (ATF-104) towed her out; new secret explosives were set off aboard: "deck and side plates
    buckled, three holes were ripped in her sides, but she wouldn't go down" (AP 1948 via JMA22).
  - The Navy had announced she would be finished off by Iowa and the cruisers Springfield, Astoria and Pasadena (USNI
    1948 via JMA22). The logs show **Iowa, Astoria and Pasadena** firing on 31 July.
  - 31 July: Astoria opened fire at 1321 (56 × 6-in, 40 × 5-in); Pasadena 57 × 6-in, 57 × 5-in; Iowa 27 × 16-in, 50 ×
    5-in: **287 rounds in about twenty minutes**, "still afloat". Also destroyers, rocket-firing planes and radar-guided
    "Bat" bombs (which missed) (AP).
  - Final blow: aerial torpedoes. Press (Wagoner 1948): "a final five aerial torpedo strikes 'opened her side and she
    sank stern first within a minute after the last one hit'". Astoria's log: capsized and sank at **1435**.
    DANFS63: "sunk by gunfire and aerial torpedoes". DNA82-2: "after four days of gunfire, bombs, rockets, and torpedoes".
    **The common "one aerial torpedo amidships" is not supported.**
  - Adm. DeWitt C. Ramsey, watching: "She was a grand old ship." Iowa's crew in dress whites held a committal service over
    the spot "66 miles southwest of Honolulu" (AP via JMA22).
- **Position.** DNA82-2: "65 miles SW of Pearl Harbor at 20-58N, 159-17W in 2600 fathoms". Computed here (great circle):
  that point is **~76 nautical miles at bearing ~254° (WSW)** from the harbour entrance, so the coordinates and the "65
  miles" do not match exactly; 2,600 fathoms (15,600 ft) does match the wreck depth. The wreck was found 65 nmi SW
  (SEARCH, JMA22). Use "about 65 nautical miles southwest of Pearl Harbor".

## E. The wreck

- **Who and how.** SEARCH Inc. and Ocean Infinity, from Ocean Infinity's ship **Pacific Constructor**, late April 2020,
  during the pandemic. A Kongsberg HUGIN AUV with synthetic-aperture sonar ran a 24-hour survey of a 10 × 10 nmi box
  centred on the positions in the logs of Iowa, Astoria and Pasadena; then an 8-hour ROV dive, with the archaeologists
  joining by video call from shore; then a second AUV pass (JMA22). The positions came from NARA logbook research in 2016
  by Pam Orlando of **NOAA's Maritime Heritage Program** (JMA22): the only NOAA involvement found. Announced 11 May 2020
  (NATGEO20 and press).
- **Depth:** 4,715 m (15,470 ft), "over 15,400 feet" (JMA22, SEARCH, NATGEO20). Ignore Fox News's "20,406 ft, RV Petrel".
- **Distance:** 65 nautical miles southwest of Pearl Harbor (SEARCH, JMA22, NATGEO20).
- **Upside down.** SEARCH: "The overturned hull was largely intact and major pieces such as turrets lay scattered in a
  vast debris field." NATGEO20: "came to rest upside down on a muddy plain". JMA22: "the overturned, main hull";
  "Both turrets are inverted, having fallen out of the barbettes as Nevada capsized"; the bow (from ~frame 40) and stern
  (from ~frame 120) are gone, the unarmoured ends of an "all or nothing" ship, likely sheared off during the fall.
  PHAM's "she landed upright" is wrong. **HIGH: upside down.**
- **Debris field:** concentrated 850 × 510 m; total about 1,470 m long; reaches ~600 m (2,000 ft) from the hull (JMA22,
  NATGEO20).
- **What the pictures show:** hull number **"36"** on the stern, and "140" (a Crossroads frame mark) (NATGEO20; JMA22
  Fig. 14); a 40-mm mount; the foremast; an overturned Crossroads test tank still tangled in its tie-down cable; armour
  plates; hints of orange paint (JMA22). Delgado sees "evidence for a second torpedo" (NATGEO20): interpretation only.
- **Rights.** All wreck stills and video are credited **"Ocean Infinity/SEARCH, Inc."** (NATGEO20 and press). They are
  **not public domain**; no licence was found. JMA22 is "© The Author(s), under exclusive licence to Springer"; PMC's
  reuse grant was tied to the COVID-19 emergency, not a CC licence. **Do not use wreck imagery without written permission
  from SEARCH/Ocean Infinity.** For the wreck act, use a map, sonar-style motion graphic or reconstruction.
- **Protection.** NHHC's SMCA page (snippet): the Sunken Military Craft Act (28 Oct 2004) keeps US Navy sunken craft US
  property; they may not be disturbed without Navy permission. Press said Nevada "will now be protected under the SMCA";
  the NHHC 2020 statement itself was not read. NHHC Director Sam Cox issued a statement (snippet). SEARCH: a Navy memorial
  fly-over followed the find; the Coast Guard dropped a wreath on the site on the 80th anniversary (7 Dec 2021).
- **Ross.** Donald Ross died 27 May 1992 (NHHC-ROSS). JMA22: the submarine **USS Nevada (SSBN-733)** scattered his ashes
  over the place where the battleship sank. Strong ending beat.

## F. Is the title true? "The Battleship That Survived Two Atomic Bombs"

- **True:** Nevada was in the target array for both Able (600 yd from the burst) and Baker (1,060 yd), and was afloat
  after both (BUAER, BS-A1, BS-B, DNA82-2, DANFS63).
- **Not unique:** BUAER's distance tables list New York and Pennsylvania in both tests too; both stayed afloat. NPS37
  App. I: New York "Sunk as target off Pearl Harbor, July 8, 1948"; Pennsylvania "Scuttled off Kwajalein, February 10,
  1948". Many other targets also survived both (e.g. Independence, Pensacola, Salt Lake City, Prinz Eugen). Arkansas
  (Baker) and Nagato (four days after Baker) were the battleships lost.
- **What is unique and true:** she was **the bull's-eye**, the ship they painted orange and aimed the first bomb at.
- **Safe wording:** "The Battleship That Survived Two Atomic Bombs" is fine as a title (it does not say "only"). In the
  script never say "the only ship" or "the only battleship" to survive both; say "the ship they aimed the bomb at".

## H. Ship basics (reference only; the video avoids a résumé up front)

Fore River Shipbuilding, Quincy MA; laid down 4 Nov 1912; launched 11 Jul 1914; commissioned 11 Mar 1916, Capt. William
S. Sims (DANFS63; BS-A1). 27,500 t (DANFS63); 29,000 t standard and 32,070 t at Crossroads (BS-A1); 583 ft (DANFS63,
BS-A1); 10 × 14-inch guns. "All or nothing" armour (JMA22). First US oil-fired battleships / first US triple turrets:
only in Wikipedia-derived pages, **UNVERIFIED**. Modernized Norfolk Aug 1927–Jan 1930 (DANFS63, NHHC-OL, JMA22).
WWI: Bantry Bay 1918, escorted Wilson's transport into Brest (DANFS63).
