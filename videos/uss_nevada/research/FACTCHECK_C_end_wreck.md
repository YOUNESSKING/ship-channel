# FACT-CHECK C: USS Nevada (BB-36), the end (1946-1948) and the wreck (2020)

Checker C (independent). Checked 17 Nov 2026 against primary/official sources read in this session: the DNA 1982
radiation report (OSTI PDF, text extracted and read), NARA catalog records 76879 and 76880 (catalog API fields), the
*Evening Star* (Washington) of 31 Jul and 1 Aug 1948 (Library of Congress OCR, AP wire text), DANFS (1963 text),
the 2022 wreck paper (full text on PMC), SEARCH Inc.'s own project page, National Geographic (11 May 2020), the Federal
Register text of 32 CFR 767 (6 Jan 2014) for the Sunken Military Craft Act. Wikipedia not used. Scope: FACT_SHEET
sections D1-D10 and E1-E10, plus the matching "Conflicts summary" rows 11-15.
Not reachable this session: history.navy.mil (503 or TLS error) and every web.archive.org copy (connection reset);
the Honolulu Star-Bulletin / Advertiser 1948 (not in Chronicling America). So: NHHC pages are checked only through
snippets and press quotes, and no Honolulu paper was read.

**Headline findings**
1. **The sinking date is NOT settled.** DANFS, NARA, DNA and the 2022 paper all say **31 July 1948**. But the AP story
   printed in the *Evening Star* dated **Sat 31 July 1948** (p. A-2, dateline "PEARL HARBOR, July 31") says the Navy
   "finally sank her **yesterday** after four-and-a-half days of trying". A 31 July sinking at 14:35 Hawaii time
   would be about 21:00 in Washington that evening, too late for an afternoon paper of that date. That points to
   **30 July** (Hawaii date), or an AP slip. The script must not hang anything on "31 July" until the ships' logs
   (NARA) or a Honolulu paper settle it. Safe wording: "the last days of July 1948".
2. **"A single torpedo amidships" has a real source, but it is the AP's last torpedo, not the only one.** AP 1948:
   "Several torpedoes hit the Nevada and she rolled with the punch. Then came one which smashed her amidships."
   The 2022 paper counts "a final five aerial torpedo strikes". Say "torpedo bombers" and "several torpedoes".
3. **Decommissioned 29 Aug 1946 AT KWAJALEIN** (DNA 1982 text, read). DANFS's "returned to Pearl Harbor to decommission"
   is wrong on place. Two other sources agree on the date (NARA authority record: 11 Mar 1916 to 29 Aug 1946).
4. **Why not scrapped: no source states the reason.** Sources say: "highly radioactive, inside and outside, and
   uninhabitable" (after Baker); the Navy took "the 1948 decision to begin sinking the most damaged, and radioactive
   target ships". The 2022 paper never says "too radioactive to scrap". Use "damaged and radioactive", and "the Navy
   chose to sink her", not "because she was too radioactive to scrap".
5. **Distance: 65 nautical miles SW of Pearl Harbor is the WRECK (SEARCH, NatGeo, 2022 paper "120 km").** It equals
   about 75 statute miles (Gizmodo/HPR "75 miles"). The 1948 log position in DNA (20 deg 58'N, 159 deg 17'W) computes
   to **76.7 nmi at 254 deg** from the harbour entrance, so the DNA's own "65 miles SW" does not match its own
   coordinates. AP 1948 said "66 miles southwest of Honolulu", UP "62 miles from Oahu". Use "about 65 nautical miles
   (75 statute miles, 120 km) southwest".
6. **"Four days" vs "four and a half days":** both are in 1948 sources (UP/AP/Evening Star caption "four days";
   AP text "four-and-a-half days"; DNA "four days"). Pick "four days" in the script or "more than four days".
7. **Arizona's guns: THREE, all in Turret 1, plus ONE Oklahoma gun in Turret 3 (1945).** Source: 2022 paper (serials
   19L4, 18L3, 17L3 from Arizona's Turret No. 2; Oklahoma 76L3). The ROV survey did not identify any barrel by serial
   number; "they are on the wreck" is the authors' inference. The NHHC "two Oklahoma guns" snippet could not be read.
8. **Wreck: found April 2020 (survey), announced Mon 11 May 2020.** The exact find date is given by no source. Depth
   4,715 m (15,470 ft) is the paper's figure; SEARCH says "over 15,400 feet". Capsized, "slightly angled to starboard";
   hull NOT intact: bow (sheared just forward of the belt) and stern (from about frame 120) lie apart in the debris
   field; **two of four turrets** found, both inverted. "36" and the Crossroads "140" are on the **port side of the
   fantail**. The "second torpedo" is NOT in the paper (only "a possible torpedo track and initial impact" on the
   starboard side); it comes from Delgado's quote to National Geographic ("might have taken two torpedoes").
9. **Wreck images: copyrighted, no free licence found.** SEARCH site: "(c) Copyright 2023 SEARCH, Inc."; photo credits
   "Ocean Infinity/SEARCH, Inc."; the paper is (c) the authors under exclusive licence to Springer, with PMC reuse
   limited "for the duration of the WHO declaration of COVID-19". No Wikimedia Commons wreck file exists. Conclusion:
   **describe only; do not show the 2020 imagery** unless written permission arrives.
10. **NARA 76879 / 76880: item-level use restriction is "Undetermined", access "Unrestricted".** The films are
    Navy-produced (NARA record group 428, Navy Department); the catalog's shot list says "see also" each other. The
    Internet Archive copies (428-npc-3646 / -3647) carry no licence statement. Treat as US-government work (PD) but log
    "NARA use restriction: Undetermined" in CREDITS.md.

## Notes for the script (end and wreck acts)
- Never use the wreck stills or ROV video; build the wreck act from a bathymetric-style map, the NARA 1948 film (silent, no sinking scene), and diagrams.
- The 31 Jul 1948 date should wait for a log or Honolulu paper. If the owner wants a date, use "31 July" only if told it is a Navy-record date and accept the risk; the AP printed 31 July as "yesterday".
- The Ramsey line, the committal service by Iowa (crew in dress whites), and "five miles deep" are all in the same AP story (ES31) and are safe primary quotes.
- Ship-name and claim hygiene: "Springfield" can be mentioned as announced, but not credited with rounds.
