## Headline findings
1. **Bomb hits: say "at least six".** Capt. Scanland's own summary: "at least six (6) bomb hits and one torpedo hit ... possibly as many as ten". NHHC's Wallin (1968) repeats "one torpedo hit and at least six bomb hits". The "two, possibly three" in DANFS is only the count *before* she sailed.
2. **Dead: "close to sixty".** Four counts exist: 50 (NHHC photo page; NPS Navy list = 50 names), 57 (NPS Navy 50 + NPS Marines 7, my own sum), 60 (DANFS63; Warfare History Network "two officers and 60 men"), 76 (a Dec 2024 DoD feature, snippet only; it counts later deaths). Wounded **109** (DANFS63) is the only wounded figure found. Do not say "50 killed" flat.
3. **Times (all official):** attack seen 0801; torpedo 0802 (AR41) but ~0810 (Wallin); **under way 0840**, unassisted (CINCPAC); **temporarily grounded ~0910** (CINCPAC); got clear 1035; **deliberately grounded 1045** at Waipio Point (CINCPAC "voluntarily grounded").
4. **"Only battleship to get under way": TRUE.** NHHC: "The only battleship able to get underway". Add: she began moving under standing fleet doctrine "before the order was cancelled" (Wallin).
5. **Beaching: reason confirmed, "who" is split.** Reason: to stop her sinking in, and blocking, the channel (DANFS63; Wallin). Wallin (NHHC) says **Rear Adm. Furlong** (on Oglala) ordered her to the Middle Loch and sent two tugs, fearing she would block the entrance channel "especially if the enemy had dropped mines". Secondary accounts say Thomas and Lt Ruff decided to nose her into the mud off Hospital Point themselves (the 0910 grounding), and Adm. Pye's signal told her not to leave the harbour. The script must not say "her crew chose to".
6. **"Only Pearl Harbor battleship at Normandy": TRUE** (derived from two official lists). Normandy battleships were USS Nevada, Texas, Arkansas and HMS Warspite, Ramillies, Rodney (Nelson held as spare). The eight at Pearl Harbor were Pennsylvania, Tennessee, California, West Virginia, Arizona, Nevada, Oklahoma, Maryland. **She was not a flagship** (Deyo's flag was in Tuscaloosa; Bryant's in Texas).
7. **Cherbourg (25 Jun 1944): Nevada was NOT hit; Texas was.** Navy's admin history: Nevada "straddled on 23 occasions ... but she was not hit"; 112 rounds of 14-in; ceased fire 1525. The 27 straddles in DANFS63 / *All Hands* belong to the whole Normandy–Cherbourg period (the Navy's own two counts for 25 June differ: 23 vs 27). Say "straddled more than twenty times".
8. **D-Day rounds:** **337 14-in** (69 AP + 268 HC) and **2,693 5-in** on 6 June only (Lundgren, from Nevada's logs). B3 is no longer unverified, but it is D-Day only, not the whole campaign.
9. **B13 is FALSE as the brief states it.** She was "eldest (by a few months)" of the *Pearl Harbor* battleships (NHHC), not the oldest US battleship in service in 1945: Arkansas (comm. 1912), Texas (Mar 1914) and New York (Apr 1914) were older and served at Okinawa.
10. **Seven stars: CONFIRMED** with the list (Wallin): Pearl Harbor–Midway, Aleutians, Normandy (including Cherbourg), Southern France, Iwo Jima, Okinawa, Third Fleet. Cherbourg is inside the Normandy star, not a separate star.
11. **Okinawa kamikaze: CONFIRMED with the Navy's own 1945 numbers:** 27 Mar, 0620, 11 killed, 49 wounded (*All Hands*); 5 Apr shore battery: 2 killed, 16 wounded, she fired 71 rounds of 14-in in 16 minutes.
12. **Band at 0800 colours: still not in any official text.** Treat as survivor memory. Even the secondary accounts disagree on whether the band stopped when strafed.
