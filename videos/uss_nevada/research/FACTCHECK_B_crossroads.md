# FACT-CHECK B: Operation Crossroads (1946) and the title claim "survived two atomic bombs"

Checker B (independent). Checked 17 Nov 2026 against primary/official sources read in full text: *Bombs at Bikini* (JTF-1 official
report, 1947), the Bureau of Ships "Technical Inspection Reports" on USS Nevada for Test Able and Test Baker (Director of Ship
Material, 1946, DTIC copies on archive.org), the Bureau of Aeronautics aircraft report (DTIC AD0367502, distance tables for every
ship), DNA 82-2 (OSTI, Crossroads participant dose summaries, one sheet per ship), NPS *Archeology of the Atomic Bomb* ch. 2 and 4
(Delgado et al.), and the National Academies/IOM 1996 volume. Wikipedia not used. DNA 6032F itself was not reachable this session
(see X rows). Scope: FACT_SHEET section C, F, the matching Conflicts rows, plus coordinator rows X1-X5.

**Headline findings**
1. **Title is TRUE; "only" is FALSE.** Nevada, New York (BB-34) and Pennsylvania (BB-38) all sit in the BUAER Able *and* Baker distance
   tables and were afloat after both. Arkansas sank in Baker; Nagato sank 29 Jul after Baker. Safe line: "She survived two atomic bombs."
2. **How far the bomb missed: say "about a third of a mile".** Sources span 1,500-2,000 ft (BAB47, 0.28-0.38 mi), 600 yd (BUAER),
   615 yd (NPS list), 710 yd (NPS text), about 1,750 ft (IOM). "About seven hundred yards" rests on **one** source (NPS) and
   contradicts NPS's own 615 yd list and BUAER's 600 yd. "More than a third of a mile" fails against BAB47's low end (1,500 ft).
   "About a third of a mile" fits every source. If a number is wanted: "roughly six hundred yards" (two official tables agree).
3. **Able: 1 Jul 1946, 09:00:34 Bikini time, air burst about 520 ft (NPS 518 ft), B-29 "Dave's Dream" No. 44-27354 from Kwajalein.**
   Yield: **23 kt** (DNA 82-2, IOM) vs **19.1 kt / about 20 kt** (NPS). Script: "about twenty kilotons" covers both.
4. **Baker: 25 Jul 1946, 08:35, 90 ft below the surface (bomb slung under LSM-60).** DNA 82-2 gives 0835 and 23 kt; NPS gives
   about 20.3 kt. Same safe wording: "about twenty kilotons". "Baker was the same size as Able" is safe.
5. **Paint: "international orange" is documented (BS-A1); "red-orange" and "scarlet" are not.** Purpose is documented: the bombardier had to
   pick out the "brightly-painted Zeropoint ship" (BAB47). The 1,500-2,000 ft miss is quoted in the same report, cause never found.
6. **Nevada animals: goats and rats in the pilot house** (BS-A1, "apparently unaffected by heat and pressure", no radiological report).
7. **Baker water column about a mile high; "millions of tons" of water.** Official figures disagree (2 million vs 10 million tons) so no
   single number.
8. **"Gilda"** is now **CONFIRMED as stated by NPS** (nickname stencilled with Rita Hayworth's likeness). It is NPS's account, not a JTF-1 document.

**Script-safe wordings**
- "She survived two atomic bombs." (true)
- "She was the ship they aimed the first bomb at, and she survived both." (true)
- "Two other American battleships, New York and Pennsylvania, also lived through both blasts." (true)
- DO NOT SAY: "the only ship / only battleship to survive two atomic bombs"; "unscathed" (BAB47: moderate damage, boilers out, fires); "the bomb missed by seven hundred yards" as flat fact; "red"; "she survived without a scratch".

## Corrections the script must apply
1. Miss distance: "about a third of a mile" (not "seven hundred yards", not "more than a third of a mile").
2. Paint: "bright orange" or "international orange"; never "red" or "red-orange".
3. Yields: "about twenty kilotons" for both Able and Baker (23 kt DNA vs 19-20 kt NPS).
4. Title: "survived two atomic bombs" is true; never "only"; name New York and Pennsylvania if the line is challenged.
5. Able sank five (Gilliam, Carlisle, Anderson, Lamson, Sakawa); Baker nine, name only Arkansas, Saratoga, Nagato (Nagato evening of 29 Jul).
6. Baker: 25 Jul, 8:35 a.m., 90 ft down; Nevada about 1,000 yd away (1,060) vs about 600 yd in Able.
7. Column: "about a mile high", "millions of tons"; no single figure.
8. Target fleet: "about ninety" (or "more than eighty"); 42,000 men, 242 ships are firm.
9. Bikini people: 162 (not 167), "March 1946"; do not give a day without the NHHC page.
10. Animals: goats and rats were in Nevada's pilot house and the report says "unaffected by heat and pressure", with no radiation result; do not claim they lived.
