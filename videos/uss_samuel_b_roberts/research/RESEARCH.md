## 0. Corrections to the brief (important for the script)

1. **No "Parry Shen".** The researcher who pinpointed the wreck was Lt Cdr Parks Stephenson. (FACT_SHEET E2)
2. **Depth: 6,895 m was the first figure, 6,865 m is Guinness's recalibrated figure.** The brief's "6,865 m / 22,621 ft" mixes them. Safe on-screen line: "almost 6,900 metres, nearly 23,000 feet". The wreck is about 400 m below Johnston.
3. **Paul Carr got the Silver Star, not the Medal of Honor.** USS Carr (FFG-52) is his ship. Only Evans (Johnston) got a Medal of Honor in Taffy 3.
4. **Hull is in two pieces (about 10 m apart), not three.**
5. **Sinking time:** 10:05 (Military Times); not 10:35. Abandon ship ~09:35 (Wikipedia), 09:10 per the Navy ensign page (snippet). Re-check.
6. **Rounds:** 608 five-inch shells (Military Times) vs "over 600 in 35 minutes" (Wikipedia). Use "more than 600".
7. **Torpedo hit on Chokai is contested** (see section 5).
8. **Quote origin:** "destroyer escort that fought like a battleship" has no traced originator; do not credit Copeland or the Navy.
9. **Still the deepest?** Nothing found since 2022 that is deeper (section 6), but I could not verify absence.

## 2. The ship, in one beat (hold the answer; no résumé in the first 2 min)

John C. Butler-class destroyer escort, built by Brown Shipbuilding in Houston, commissioned 28 April 1944 (DANFS). About 306 ft and 1,350 tons; two 5-inch guns and a triple torpedo mount (FACT_SHEET A5-A9). Named for Coxswain Samuel Booker Roberts Jr., 21 when he died on 28 Sep 1942, posthumous Navy Cross for rescuing Marines on Guadalcanal under fire (DANFS). Copeland was a Tacoma lawyer and reservist (Wikipedia). She was in service six months before the battle.

## 3. The battle (what is solid)

- Taffy 3: six escort carriers, three destroyers, four destroyer escorts, Rear Adm Clifton Sprague, off Samar, 25 Oct 1944. Kurita's force: four battleships (including Yamato, Kongo), six heavy cruisers, two light cruisers, 11 destroyers.
- Copeland: "We're making a torpedo run. The outcome is doubtful, but we will do our duty." Closed to about 4,000 yards of Chokai, three torpedoes (Military Times). Navy Cross citation (as paraphrased): he "diverted enemy fire to himself" from the ships he protected.
- Roberts then fought on for about an hour with her 5-inch guns: 608 rounds (Military Times); gun 2 over 300; star shells included; Carr loaded to the end.
- About 09:00, Kongo's 14-inch fire: two or three hits, a hole 40 x 10 ft in the port side, an engine room destroyed. Dead in the water, about 20 hits taken. She was abandoned about 09:35 and sank about 10:05. 89 (or 90) died; about 120 survived on three rafts for about 50 hours, rescued 27 Oct (26 Oct per a NARA caption).
- Kurita turned away about 09:20 (Wikipedia). Taffy 3 lost Gambier Bay, St. Lo, Hoel, Johnston, Roberts; 1,161 killed and missing. Taffy 3 got the Presidential Unit Citation; Evans the Medal of Honor posthumously; 22 Navy Crosses (NHHC via snippet).

## 4. Scale: the "wow"

Roberts: 306 ft, 1,350 tons, 5-inch guns (127 mm). Yamato: 862 ft 10 in, 64,000 long tons normal, nine 18.1-inch (460 mm) guns, range 26 miles. So the little ship was about a third of Yamato's length, about one-fiftieth of her weight at full load, and the guns were a quarter of the calibre. Yamato did fire on her (Wikipedia: Yamato, Nagato, Haruna also fired), but no source says who hit. Kongo's 14-inch fire is the documented killer.
Other early wow candidates: (a) a destroyer escort ordered to charge a battleship fleet; (b) the wreck lies deeper than any ship on Earth ever found, 98 percent of the ocean is shallower (Smithsonian); (c) the depth charges are still in their racks (Maritime Executive); (d) hull number "413" still readable (Divernet).

## 5. The torpedo question (candidate reversal)

- Popular story (WarHistoryOnline; Wikipedia ship page "credited"): torpedoes hit Chokai.
- DANFS: "a torpedo hit on one" cruiser.
- Wikipedia ship page body: a lookout reported a hit, but "Chokai was not hit by a torpedo". The Chokai article does not mention Roberts, and a Wikipedia talk page notes the same.
- Military Times 2025: all three missed; a 5-inch shell hit on Chokai's torpedo battery caused the explosion that crippled her. Navy page (snippet): chance hit from White Plains.
- Verdict: **not settled in what I could read.** Write the script line as "fired three torpedoes at Chokai", then let the narration say what the Navy believed in 1944 and what historians say now. Needs the action report and Hornfischer's notes.

## 6. Discovery and record status

- Vescovo (Caladan Oceanic) + EYOS Expeditions, DSV Limiting Factor, six dives over eight days 17-24 June 2022 (Divernet); Vescovo's own dive 22 June with Jeremie Morizet. Stephenson's archival research moved the search off the recorded position. Two dives below 7 km had failed to find Gambier Bay (Divernet).
- Found: two pieces on a slope, ~10 m apart; bow buckled from impact; stern separated ~5 m; hull number 413, mast, aft gun mount, pilot-house, three-tube torpedo launcher (Divernet says Roberts was the only sunken warship of its type armed so), depth charges in racks; shell holes and a "massive" hit on the stern (Smithsonian paraphrase).
- Depth: 6,895 m (22,621 ft) per Vescovo; 6,865 m (22,523 ft) per Guinness after recalibration; Wikipedia 6,890 m. Smithsonian's "22,916 ft" is inconsistent and should not be used.
- Prior record: Johnston, 21,180 ft (6,456 m) per Caladan (others 6,469-6,470 m), confirmed 2021; first glimpsed in 2019 (about 20,400 ft). Johnston's own wording: "the world's deepest known shipwreck", "the deepest wreck dives, manned or unmanned, in history".
- Wording by source: Vescovo "deepest shipwreck ever located and surveyed"; Guinness "deepest shipwreck found to date" and "deepest shipwreck dive by a crewed vessel"; Smithsonian "the deepest ever found"; CBS "deepest shipwreck ever located"; Maritime Executive "deepest shipwreck found to date". **Safest script wording: "the deepest shipwreck ever found, and the deepest ever surveyed by a crewed submersible".** Note a stricter claim ("deepest wreck ever located" by sonar, unmanned) could be beaten by an undetected unmanned find.
- 2023-2026 check: searches (standard and extended) found **no deeper wreck**; The Scuba News (4 Nov 2025) still calls Roberts the deepest "almost 6,900 metres"; Guinness page lists it. Nothing found on Ocean Infinity, Japanese or other expeditions beating it. **Absence of results is not proof**; Gambier Bay (Vescovo failed twice below 7 km) is an obvious future competitor. Re-run a news search the week of publishing.
- Legal: Sunken Military Craft Act; site is a war grave; Navy "no plans to disturb". Vescovo's data goes to NHHC. Do not show exact coordinates (none found anyway).

## 7. Reversal candidates

1. The torpedo "hit" that made her famous may not have happened (section 5). Strongest mid-video reversal, fits "numbers land on spoken words" (three torpedoes, 4,000 yards).
2. The record itself: Johnston held it for 15 months; and the first depth for Roberts was revised down by 30 m.
3. The Navy's own wording is "hallowed war grave", not "wreck". Use for the ending tone.
4. The kill: not Yamato but Kongo's 14-inch (the shells were smaller than Yamato's 18.1-inch).
