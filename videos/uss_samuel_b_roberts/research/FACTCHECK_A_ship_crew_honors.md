## Headline findings (script-safe wording)
1. **Carr: Silver Star, not Medal of Honor. CONFIRMED.** Valor database (Military Times): posthumous Silver Star, GM3c, USNR, action 25 Oct 1944, gun captain of a 5"/38 mount.
   Citation summary: rammer lost and hoist failed, he held station under close-range fire, his skill helped the gun damage an enemy heavy cruiser; mortally wounded by a **premature
   powder-charge detonation**, he tried alone to load and ram the last available projectile, even after the abandon-ship order; "He gallantly gave his life for his country."
   Oklahoma HOF: born 13 Feb 1924 Checotah OK; USS Carr (FFG-52) named in his honor 27 Jul 1985 (the fact sheet's "Checotah" is right). Safe: "Gunner's Mate Paul Carr kept loading after the
   hoist failed, and died trying to ram the last round. He got the Silver Star." **Mount number is a conflict (No. 2 / "Mount 52" / "#4")**: say "the aft five-inch gun" only if the action report confirms aft; Wikipedia, MT25 say Mount 2 (aft in the fact sheet, not verified by me).
   "Died after being dragged from his turret" (MT25 per fact sheet) is NOT in the citation; use the citation wording (mortally wounded, kept trying to load). Do not say "gripping the last round" as fact: the MOAA/HOF wording is secondary; the citation says "tried unassisted to load and ram".
2. **Casualties: say "89 men died in the battle's aftermath" only with the sourcing; safest is "about 90 of her crew died".** 89 appears in Navy Memorial log and NavSource (via search); Wikipedia says 89 killed plus 25 died later of wounds/shark attack (arithmetic does not close: Wikipedia itself flags this). I found no official DANFS count (the DANFS text I read gives NO casualty number). Survivors "120" is consistent in DANFS, Navy Memorial, Wikipedia. Safe: "of about 224 men aboard, 120 survived".
3. **Rescue date: UNRESOLVED (26 vs 27 Oct).** DANFS says survivors "clung to 3 life rafts for 50 hours" with no date; Wikipedia: no date, "about 50 hours"; a Navy/NARA photo caption (via search) says 26 Oct; fact sheet's 27 Oct comes from Wikipedia Samar. 25 Oct ~09:35-10:05 + 50 h = ~noon-midday 27 Oct, but the Navy Memorial says the rescue came "at the start of their third day", which is consistent with early 27 Oct only if "day 1" is 25 Oct. Safe: **"after about 50 hours in the water"** and never a calendar date on screen.
2b. **Rescuers: "a patrol craft escorting a group of five LCIs" (DANFS paraphrase via Wikipedia; Navy Memorial: "a PC escorting five LCIs").** I could NOT confirm the fact sheet's idea of other named ships. Do not name the PC (commonly cited as PC-1119 in books, unverified by me). Safe: "a patrol craft and its landing-craft convoy found them".
4. **"Roberts was hit by a torpedo"**: DANFS (old text via ibiblio) really does say "hitting one with a torpedo and scoring at least 40 gunfire hits on another"; the Navy Memorial says "at least one hit". So the Navy's own historic text supports a hit; modern accounts dispute it. Keep the fact sheet's attribution wording.
5. **Builder/dates CONFIRMED:** Brown Shipbuilding, Houston; keel 6 Dec 1943; launched 20 Jan 1944 (sponsor Mrs. Samuel B. Roberts, the namesake's mother per DANFS wording "Mrs. Samuel B. Roberts"); commissioned 28 Apr 1944, Lt Cdr R. W. Copeland USNR commanding (DANFS via ibiblio + Wikipedia).
6. **Complement/armament: CORRECTED. DANFS says crew 222 (design, listed), Wikipedia class complement 14 officers + 201 enlisted = 215; "224 aboard" is the press/Wikipedia count for 25 Oct.** Use "about 220 men". Armament per DANFS: two 5-inch, **four** 40 mm, ten 20 mm, **three 21-inch torpedo tubes**, depth-charge projectors/throwers, Hedgehog. Wikipedia says 40 mm in two twin mounts (= four guns) so it agrees. The fact sheet's "one triple mount" = three tubes. Fine.
7. **PUC/battle star: CONFIRMED.** DANFS: included in the PUC to TU 77.4.3 and one battle star. destroyerhistory.org PUC page lists Samuel B. Roberts among the ships, signed by SecNav Forrestal; "For extraordinary heroism in action against powerful units of the Japanese Fleet during the Battle off Samar, Philippines, October 25, 1944" (via search snippet of NHHC page). Also the Philippine Republic PUC.
8. **Copeland: CONFIRMED with detail.** b. 9 Sep 1910 Tacoma WA, d. 25 Aug 1973 Tacoma. Practised law (1935-40, resumed after the war). Commissioned USNR 1935, active duty 1940. Prior commands: Pawtucket (YT-7), Black Douglas (PYc-45), Wyman (DE-38). Navy Cross, Valor database: Lt Cdr, USNR, action 25 Oct 1944, DE-413; opening of citation "for extraordinary heroism and distinguished service in the line of his profession as Commanding Officer of the Destroyer Escort U.S.S. Samuel B. Roberts". Retired rear admiral (USNR). Safe: "Lieutenant Commander Robert Copeland, a Naval Reserve officer and a lawyer from Tacoma, not an Annapolis man."
   (The "not Annapolis" part: he came up through the Reserve, per Wikipedia; I did not find an official statement that he did not attend the Academy; drop it if unsure.)
9. **Namesake: CONFIRMED, one correction.** Samuel Booker Roberts Jr., b. 12 May 1921 San Francisco; enlisted USNR 13 Apr 1939, Portland OR (DANFS via MaritimeQuest); coxswain on a Higgins boat from USS Bellatrix (AK-20) (Valor/Home of Heroes). **Date of action 27 Sep 1942; death 28 Sep 1942** (Valor says action 27 Sep; DANFS says death 28 Sep; Roberts was hit in the neck at the end of the evacuation and died the next day, per the Coast Guard/other accounts in search summary). Place: Point Cruz beachhead, Guadalcanal, evacuating Marines (1st Bn, 7th Marines, Cos A and B). Posthumous Navy Cross, "extraordinary heroism and devotion to duty" (Valor/Home of Heroes; the DANFS paraphrase says "while serving on the crew of a landing craft"). He volunteered knowing the boat would draw fire away from the other boats (Valor summary). Safe: "Coxswain Samuel Roberts, 21, died saving Marines on Guadalcanal; the Navy named a ship for him." Age at death: 21 (12 May 1921 to Sep 1942).
10. **Later ships: CONFIRMED.** DD-823 (Samuel B. Roberts II) and FFG-58 (Samuel B. Roberts III), commissioned 12 Apr 1986. **FFG-58 hit an Iranian M-08 moored contact mine, 14 Apr 1988, central Persian Gulf, just before 5 pm** (Wikipedia, USNI/NHHC via search). Ten sailors medevaced for blast injuries, none killed (Wikipedia); she returned to service 16 Oct 1989 after 13 months' repair; led to Operation Praying Mantis (18 Apr 1988). Decommissioned 22 May 2015; scrapping claim weakly sourced. DE-413 has no ship that outlasted her except by name.
11. **Book: CONFIRMED.** *The Spirit of the Sammy-B* by Rear Adm Robert W. Copeland with Jack E. O'Neill, ~120 pages, softcover via Lulu (de413.org book page); a "first hand account of the Battle of Leyte Gulf". Publication year not found. Safe: "Copeland wrote it down himself in a short memoir." Do not give a year.
12. **Copeland quote to crew (B4)**: MOAA page quotes Copeland: "We are about to go into a fight against overwhelming odds from which survival cannot be expected." That is the wording that appears in several Navy-history retellings and matches Wikipedia's 1MC version, not the Military Times "outcome is doubtful" line. Prefer the "overwhelming odds from which survival cannot be expected" wording; both unverified against the action report.
