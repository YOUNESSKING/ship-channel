# FACT-CHECK B: Battle off Samar, 25 Oct 1944, and the Roberts' part in it (FACT_SHEET B, G)

Checker B (independent). Checked 19 Nov 2026.

**Limits up front.** history.navy.mil (DANFS, H-Gram special edition Samar, Samar page, ensign page), usni.org, nationalinterest.org and silo.pub
(Hornfischer text) all returned 503/403 or failed. **Copeland's Combined Action Report and Sprague's report were not found as text anywhere.**
What I could read directly:
- ibiblio HyperWar: the Navy's own contemporaneous *Battle Experience: Battle of Leyte Gulf* (Secret Information Bulletin 22, Leyte-BE-78.1) and
  the TF-77 preliminary report. These are Navy primary-ish documents. I read only characters 0-100000 of 78.1. The Samar narrative itself (p. 25) was not reached.
- Old DANFS on ibiblio, historyofwar.org, destroyerhistory.org, Navy Memorial Navy Log (a 1944 Navy press release retyped), de413.org (the same release),
  Military Times 2025, WarHistoryOnline, and Wikipedia (Samar, DE-413, Copeland, Yamato).
- Wikipedia is used only where it quotes a primary (Copeland's report p.14, the Navy Cross citation). It is marked "WIKI".
- Everything from Hornfischer, Morison or the NHHC pages is second-hand via search snippets and is marked SNIP.

## Headline findings (script-safe wording)

1. **The two Copeland quotes are NOT both from the same place, and only one traces to the action report. CORRECTED.**
   - Wikipedia's Copeland page cites the *Combined Action Report* (20 Nov 1944, Serial X001, p.14). It says the crew heard over the loudspeaker
     "a fight against overwhelming odds from which survival could not be expected." This is a paraphrase inside the report, not a verbatim speech transcript.
     The DE-413 page gives it as "This will be a fight against overwhelming odds from which survival cannot be expected. We will do what damage we can."
   - "We're making a torpedo run. The outcome is doubtful, but we will do our duty." appears in Military Times, WarHistoryOnline and Yahoo.
     No primary source for it was found.
   - **Script: "Copeland told his crew over the loudspeaker that this would be a fight against overwhelming odds, and that survival could not be expected."**
     Use the "overwhelming odds" line, which has the report behind it. Use "outcome is doubtful" only on screen as "reported", or leave it out.
2. **Did a torpedo hit? CONTESTED. Do not state either way.** The 1944 Navy press release and the old DANFS say "a torpedo hit" ("One torpedo hit and started fires").
   Military Times 2025 says all three missed and a 5-inch shell hit Chokai's torpedo battery. Wikipedia says a lookout reported a hit but Chokai was not hit by a torpedo.
   Wikipedia's Samar page says Japanese reports do not support the claim. SNIP: Hornfischer says Chokai's end "remains largely a mystery",
   and the NHHC H-Gram credits a White Plains shell for the torpedo-battery explosion.
   **Script: "Roberts fired all three torpedoes at the heavy cruiser Chokai from about 4,000 yards. Her crew believed one hit. Japanese records do not back that up."**
3. **Target.** The Navy release says only "a Japanese heavy cruiser". Chokai is the usual identification (Wikipedia, MT25, Hornfischer via SNIP: "probably Chokai").
   **Script: "a heavy cruiser, the Chokai by most accounts."** Do not name Chikuma as the torpedo target. Chikuma was the later gun duel (Wikipedia).
4. **Torpedo-run time: CONFLICT. UNVERIFIED.** WIKI-DE413 says Copeland turned about 07:40 (a search summary of the Copeland page says 07:35).
   The Navy's own Battle Experience bulletin says "at about 0730" Hoel, Johnston and Roberts "reversed and delivered a daylight half-salvo torpedo attack against the enemy battleships",
   and it gives no Roberts-specific time. **Script: "Around 7:30 to 7:40."** Do not use 08:45.
   The fact sheet's "08:45" is the Wikipedia Samar page's later time for Roberts engaging, not the torpedo run.
5. **Sighting and first fire.** The contemporaneous Navy bulletin: AA fire reported at 0646, large enemy fleet sighted seven minutes later (about 0653, the fetch tool's calculation, not stated).
   Wikipedia: Ensign Brooks sighted ships 06:37, tactical surprise 06:45. The Navy text gives no exact fire time ("Almost immediately after being sighted the enemy opened fire").
   Wikipedia Yamato page: Yamato opened fire at 06:57. Historyofwar.org: 6:58. Wikipedia Samar: "about 07:00" at 17 nmi.
   **Script: "Just before seven, Yamato's 18.1-inch guns opened fire from about 20 miles."** Say "within minutes of first contact."
6. **Kurita's strength: the "11 destroyers" is Wikipedia's figure; the Navy's own count differed.** Wikipedia: 4 BB, 6 CA, 2 CL, 11 DD. Historyofwar: "four battleships, six cruisers and ten destroyers".
   The Navy's TF-77 report estimated 4 BB, 5 CA, 1 CL, 11 DD. Its Bulletin 22 post-battle analysis: "5 BBs, 7 CAs, 1 CL and 13 DDs" for the whole Center Force
   and says reports of extra CLs "cannot be reconciled". **Script: "four battleships, six heavy cruisers and about a dozen destroyers."** Avoid exact destroyer and light-cruiser counts.
7. **Kurita's turn-away: 09:11 (historyofwar), 09:20 (Wikipedia Samar), about 09:24 (Navy bulletin: enemy cruisers "ceased fire, countermarched in column to the left and retired to the northeast"), 09:30 (CIMSEC, Wikipedia lead).**
   **Script: "At about 9:20, with the carriers still afloat, Kurita turned away."** Say "a little after nine". Do not give a minute.
8. **Roberts' sinking time: NOT in any primary found. 10:05 is MT25's figure only.** Abandon order: DANFS (NHHC, SNIP) says 0910. Wikipedia and WarHistoryOnline say 09:35, MT25 about 09:37.
   Sinking: Wikipedia is "30 minutes later" (about 10:05). **Script: "She sank a little after ten o'clock."** Do not give an abandon-ship minute.
9. **Taffy 3 US dead: no source agrees. UNVERIFIED.** Wikipedia infobox 1,161 killed/missing (913 wounded). Military Wiki, National Interest: 1,583 killed/missing.
   HistoryCentral: 1,130. Wikipedia's aftermath text: "over 1,500". The fact sheet's "~1,100" is the low end. **Script: "more than a thousand American sailors died."**
10. **608 rounds: only MT25. Contradicted in scope.** Wikipedia: "over 600 rounds in about 35 minutes". The 1944 Navy release says one 5-inch gun fired "more than 300 rounds" in about 50 minutes, with "at least 40 sure hits".
    **Script: "She fired more than six hundred 5-inch shells."** Do not say 608 or "in an hour".
11. **"Fought like a battleship": still no originator.** MT25: "known ever after as". No source ties it to Copeland or the Navy. Do not attribute.
12. **Scale (CONFIRMED, Wikipedia only).** Yamato 862 ft 10 in overall, 64,000 long tons normal / 70,527 full load, nine 18.1-inch guns. Roberts 306 ft, 1,350 tons, two 5-inch.
    Wikipedia also says Yamato's "only surface action" was Samar, and that she is credited with Taffy 3's sinkings except Roberts.
    That last point matters. **Yamato did not hit Roberts. Script: "Yamato was in that battle, but it was Kongo's 14-inch guns that sank Roberts."**
