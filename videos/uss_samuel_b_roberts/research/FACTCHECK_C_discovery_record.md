## Headline findings (script-safe wording)
1. **Still the record as of 19 Nov 2026: YES, as far as can be found.** Guinness "Deepest shipwreck" page says "The deepest
   shipwreck found to date is the USS Samuel B. Roberts" (and warns "Records change on a daily basis and are not immediately
   published online"). Extended and standard searches for 2023-2026 (Ocean Infinity, Vescovo, Japan, Pacific Wrecks, aircraft)
   found NO deeper wreck. A Feb 2026 French find (16th-century ship, 2,567 m) is a French-waters record only. Absence of
   results is not proof: re-run a search in the week of upload.
2. **Safe phrasing: "the deepest shipwreck ever found" is allowed, dated:** "In June 2022 she became the deepest shipwreck ever
   located, and she still holds that record." Best exact-wording version (Vescovo's own): "the deepest shipwreck ever located and
   surveyed". Avoid "dived" for the ship (people dive; the Guinness title is "Deepest shipwreck dive by a crewed vessel").
3. **Depth: say "almost 6,900 metres, about four miles down" (or "nearly 23,000 feet").** 6,895 m is the first announcement
   (Vescovo, Navy release, press); 6,865 m / 22,523 ft is Guinness after recalibration. Both round to "almost 6,900 m"; if one number is
   shown on screen use "6,865 m (Guinness)" or "6,895 m (first announced)" with the label. Never mix 6,865 m with 22,621 ft.
4. **Dives: six dives over eight days, 17-24 June 2022 (Divernet); Vescovo's dive on the Roberts 22 June 2022 (Divernet, Guinness).**
5. **Navy: "hallowed war grave" CONFIRMED (Cox); Sunken Military Craft Act CONFIRMED.**
6. **Parry Shen: no source. Historian is Lt Cdr Parks Stephenson (ret.)** (Divernet).
7. **Location: Philippine Sea, off Samar. "Philippine Trench" is NOT in the sources I could open for the Roberts**; Wikipedia's Johnston
   page says Johnston was "in the Philippine Trench", and one search summary put Roberts there, unconfirmed. Say "off Samar, in the Philippine Sea".
   Exact coordinates are not public.
8. **"Two pieces about 10 m apart" CONFIRMED by Divernet, but Vescovo's own words are "stern also separated about 5 meters... the whole wreck
   was together" (Stripes, CBS, MarEx).** Say "broken in two", not a distance, or say "a few metres apart".
