# FACT SHEET: USS Samuel B. Roberts (DE-413), "The Deepest Shipwreck Ever Found" (working title)

Confidence: **HIGH** = two or more reliable independent sources agree, at least one official/primary ·
**MED** = one official source, or reliable sources that differ in detail · **UNVERIFIED** = fewer than two reliable
sources, or only press/secondary/estimate. **Nothing here has passed the 3-checker gate yet (CLAUDE.md).**
Narrative and reasoning: `RESEARCH.md`. Compiled 2026-11-19.

**READ FIRST: corrections to the brief.**
1. **"Parry Shen" is not in any source.** No source names a historian by that name (Parry Shen is a film actor). The archival researcher who located the wreck was **Lt Cdr Parks Stephenson (USN ret.)**, Caladan Oceanic affiliate (DIVER14). Do not use Parry Shen.
2. **The depth has THREE figures, and the one in the brief is the odd one out.** Vescovo's first post: **6,895 m (22,621 ft)**. Guinness (after "fine-tuning of the data"): **6,865 m (22,523 ft)**, i.e. 30 m shallower. Wikipedia: 3,770 fathoms / 22,620 ft / 6,890 m. The brief's "6,865 m / 22,621 ft" mixes the Guinness metres with the original feet. Pick one and say "almost 6,900 m / nearly 23,000 ft" on screen unless a checker confirms a final Caladan figure. Smithsonian's "22,916 ft" and a search snippet's "6,985 m" are errors (inconsistent with 6,895 m).
3. **Paul Carr did NOT get the Medal of Honor.** He received a posthumous **Silver Star**; USS Carr (FFG-52) is named for him (SNIP; WHO14; MT25). The only Medal of Honor at Samar in Taffy 3 was **Cdr Ernest E. Evans (Johnston)**. Carr's death cause is also loose: "died after being dragged from his turret" (MT25) vs "mortally wounded by a premature powder-charge detonation" (SNIP).
4. **Roberts' torpedo hit on Chokai is NOT established.** DANFS (via ibiblio) says "a torpedo hit on one [cruiser] and at least 40 gunfire hits on a second". Military Times/Yahoo (2025) says all three torpedoes missed from ~4,000 yd, and a 5-inch shell hit on Chokai's torpedo battery crippled her. NHHC (via search snippet) credits a chance White Plains hit. Say "fired three torpedoes at Chokai; credited with a hit by the ship's official history, disputed by modern accounts". Never state it flat.
5. **Casualty arithmetic does not close.** 224 aboard; 89 (or 90) died; 120 survivors: 224-120 = 104, not 89. Sources mix "killed in battle" with later deaths from wounds/sharks. See section D. Use "89 died" only with the source named, and "about 120 survivors".
6. **"The deepest" is dated.** No wreck deeper than Roberts found in any 2022-2026 search result (see section F). It is still the record as far as these searches show, but absence of results is not proof. "Deepest ever located and surveyed" is Vescovo's exact wording; the Guinness wording is "deepest shipwreck found to date" and "deepest shipwreck dive by a crewed vessel".
7. **Sinking time: 10:05 is one popular-press time, not an official one.** Wikipedia gives abandon-ship ~09:35 and sinking "30 minutes later" (~10:05). Navy ensign page (snippet) says abandon order at 0910. Kurita broke off at 09:20 per Wikipedia (not 09:11). Re-check the action report.
8. **"Destroyer escort that fought like a battleship" has no found originator.** One book excerpt (Wukovits) attributes praise to an unnamed deputy CNO; a 2022 columnist could not trace it. Vescovo quoted it in 2022. Do not attribute to Copeland or the Navy.
9. **Brief's "Kongo 14-inch hit" is supported; "Yamato hit her" is not.** Kongo is credited with the killing hits (Wikipedia, Military Times, DANFS says "14-inch"). Wikipedia says Yamato, Nagato and Haruna also fired at her but not who hit.
